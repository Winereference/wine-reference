Ica Valley: Peruvian pisco

Brandy · Peru

Ica is the heart of Peruvian pisco, an unaged grape brandy named after the nearby port of Pisco.

SpiritPeruvian pisco
Made fromGrapes such as Quebranta, Italia and Torontel
TasteClean and aromatic: grape, citrus, flowers and a little earth.
How to drinkIn a Pisco Sour or a Chilcano with ginger ale and lime.

How it's made

Distilled once to bottling strength with no water added, then rested at least three months in neutral vessels, never in wood.

Fresh grape must is fermented, often naturally, and distilled once in copper pot stills. The distiller stops at bottling strength, so nothing can be added afterwards, not even water.

History

Spanish settlers planted vines in Peru's coastal valleys in the 1500s, and grape brandy shipped through the port of Pisco took its name. Peru declared pisco a Denominación de Origen in 1991.

Ageing and label terms

Pisco rests at least three months in vessels that give no flavour, such as stainless steel, glass or clay, and never in wood. Labels show the style: Puro (one grape), Acholado (a blend) or Mosto Verde (distilled before fermentation has finished).

Styles

Non-aromatic piscos from grapes like Quebranta, earthy and clean, and aromatic ones from Italia, Torontel or Moscatel, full of flowers and citrus.

Rules and protected names

Only eight grape varieties are allowed, and pisco must be made in the coastal valleys of Lima, Ica, Arequipa, Moquegua and Tacna. It is bottled between 38% and 48% abv.

How to serve and classic cocktails

The Pisco Sour with lime, sugar, egg white and Angostura bitters; the Chilcano with ginger ale and lime; and the old San Francisco Pisco Punch.

With food

Ceviche, tiradito, causa and other Peruvian seafood dishes.

Good to know

Peru and Chile both make pisco and have long argued about who owns the name; some countries recognise only Peru's.

Producers

  • Pisco Portón
  • Tacama
  • Santiago Queirolo

Bottles to try

Brandy · Peru

Pisco Portón Mosto Verde

Fragrant grape, jasmine, citrus and white pepper.

Serve: In a Pisco Sour

Brandy · Peru

BarSol Primero Quebranta

Earthy and rounded with banana, green apple and a nutty, grape-skin finish.

Age and cask: Unaged, rested in neutral vessels

Made at Bodega San Isidro in Ica from the non-aromatic Quebranta grape, distilled once in copper pot stills to proof with no added water, as Peruvian pisco rules demand.

Serve: Shake into a Pisco Sour or sip neat at cellar temperature

Brandy · Peru

Caravedo Puro Quebranta

Earthy and grapey, with banana, nuts and a dry, peppery finish.

Age and cask: Unaged (rested in neutral vessels)

Made at Hacienda La Caravedo in Ica, founded in 1684 and said to be the oldest working distillery in the Americas. Single-distilled to proof from the non-aromatic Quebranta grape, with nothing added, as Peruvian rules require.

Serve: In a Pisco Sour, or neat

Brandy · Peru

Caravedo Acholado

Floral and fruity: grape, citrus, white flowers and a peppery finish.

Age and cask: Rested at least 3 months, no wood

A blend of Quebranta, Torontel and Italia grapes from Hacienda La Caravedo in Ica, founded in 1684 and one of the oldest distilleries in the Americas. Pisco is distilled once to proof with nothing added.

Serve: In a Pisco Sour
