Guizhou: Baijiu (sauce aroma)

Anise & others · China

The town of Maotai in Guizhou province is home to Kweichow Moutai, China's most famous baijiu.

SpiritBaijiu (sauce aroma)
Made fromSorghum and wheat
TasteIntense and savoury: soy sauce, roasted grains, dried fruit and earth.
How to drinkNeat in tiny glasses, shared at banquets with toasts.

How it's made

Made with solid-state fermentation in pits and many rounds of distillation over about a year, then aged for years in clay jars.

Local red sorghum is cooked and fermented in the solid state with a wheat-based starter called qu, in stone-lined pits. The grain is distilled, re-fermented and distilled again in many rounds over about a year, with each batch kept separate.

History

Spirits have been made around Maotai on the Chishui river for centuries, and Kweichow Moutai became China's best-known baijiu after it was served at state banquets in the 20th century. Guizhou is now the heartland of the sauce aroma style.

Ageing and label terms

The spirits from each round are aged separately in clay jars, usually for several years, before a master blender combines them. Labels mostly show brand, aroma type and strength rather than an age.

Styles

Sauce aroma (jiangxiang) is the signature style, savoury and rich; other Chinese styles include strong aroma from Sichuan and light aroma from Shanxi.

Rules and protected names

Moutai and the Maotai area have Chinese geographical-indication protection. Baijiu is usually bottled at about 50–55%.

How to serve and classic cocktails

Served at room temperature in tiny glasses and drunk in one go after a toast, often several times through a meal. Bartenders outside China sometimes use small amounts in sours and highballs.

With food

Made for rich Chinese banquet food: braised pork, spicy Sichuan and Guizhou dishes, roast duck and cured meats.

Good to know

Baijiu is the world's most-consumed spirit by volume, though little is drunk outside China. The aroma is intense, so it is worth tasting slowly at first.

Producers

  • Kweichow Moutai
  • Xijiu

Bottles to try

Anise & others · China

Kweichow Moutai Flying Fairy

Intense soy, roasted grain, ripe fruit and earthy funk.

Strength: 53% · Age and cask: Aged in clay jars for several years before blending

China's most famous baijiu, made in Maotai town from sorghum and wheat in long solid-state fermentations. The grain goes through many rounds of fermenting and distilling over about a year, creating the 'sauce aroma' style.

Serve: Neat at room temperature in tiny cups, with a meal

Anise & others · China

Guizhou Xijiu Junpin Xijiu

Savoury soy and roasted grain with dried fruit, honey and a long, earthy finish.

Strength: 53%

Xijiu is made in Xishui on the Chishui river, just upstream from Maotai, in the same sauce aroma style of many fermentations and distillations over a year. Junpin is its top-selling premium bottling.

Serve: Neat at room temperature in tiny glasses

Kweichow Moutai Moutai Prince

Sauce-aroma style: soy, roasted grain, dried fruit and a savoury, long finish.

Strength: 53%

A more affordable sauce-aroma baijiu from the Moutai group in Guizhou, made from sorghum with repeated fermentations and distillations.
