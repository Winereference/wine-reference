Japan: Japanese whisky

Whisky · Japan

Japanese whisky began in the 1920s, inspired by Scotch, and is now prized for its precision and balance.

SpiritJapanese whisky
Made fromMalted barley and other grains
TasteClean and balanced: orchard fruit, honey, sandalwood and incense notes from mizunara.
How to drinkNeat, or as a mizuwari or highball with plenty of ice.

How it's made

Industry standards from 2021 say whisky labelled Japanese must be distilled, aged at least three years and bottled in Japan. Some distilleries use local mizunara oak.

The method follows Scotch: malted barley (much of it imported) is fermented and distilled twice in copper pot stills, alongside column-still grain whisky for blends. Because Japanese companies rarely trade whisky with each other, big distilleries run many still shapes and yeasts to make different styles in-house.

History

Shinjiro Torii of Suntory opened Yamazaki, Japan's first malt whisky distillery, in 1923, with Masataka Taketsuru, who had studied distilling in Scotland. Taketsuru later founded Nikka and its Yoichi distillery in Hokkaido in 1934.

Ageing and label terms

Ex-bourbon, sherry and wine casks are used, plus native Mizunara oak, which gives sandalwood and incense notes. Strong demand has made many bottlings no-age-statement.

Styles

Elegant blends (Hibiki), fruity single malts (Yamazaki, Hakushu), heavier and peaty malts (Yoichi) and soft grain whiskies.

Rules and protected names

Since April 2024, members of the Japan Spirits & Liqueurs Makers Association must follow a standard for 'Japanese whisky': fermented, distilled and aged at least 3 years in wood in Japan, using malted grain and Japanese water, bottled in Japan at 40% ABV or more.

How to serve and classic cocktails

Very popular as a highball with soda or as mizuwari (with water and ice). Also used in a Japanese-style Old Fashioned.

With food

Sushi, yakitori, tempura, grilled fish and light Japanese snacks.

Good to know

Before the 2024 standard, some bottles labelled Japanese whisky contained imported whisky, so check for the JSLMA wording on the label.

Producers

  • Suntory (Yamazaki, Hakushu)
  • Nikka (Yoichi, Miyagikyo)
  • Chichibu

Bottles to try

Whisky · Japan

Suntory Hibiki Japanese Harmony

Blended and silky: honey, orange peel, rose and sandalwood.

Serve: Neat or as a highball

Whisky · Japan

Nikka From the Barrel

Rich and spicy: toffee, dark fruit, vanilla and pepper.

Strength: 51.4% · Age and cask: No age statement, blend married again in casks

A blend of malt and grain whiskies, launched in 1985 in a small square bottle. After blending, the whisky goes back into casks for a few months so the parts settle together, and it is bottled at a high strength.

Serve: Neat or with a large ice cube

Whisky · Japan

Suntory Yamazaki 12 Year Old

Peach, pineapple, honey, coconut and incense-like oak.

Strength: 43% · Age and cask: 12 years, including Mizunara (Japanese oak) casks

From Japan's first malt whisky distillery, built near Kyoto in 1923. Part of the whisky ages in Japanese Mizunara oak, which gives sandalwood and incense notes.

Serve: Neat or with a little water

Whisky · Japan

Nikka Coffey Grain

Soft and sweet: vanilla, coconut, tropical fruit and caramel.

Strength: 45% · Age and cask: No age statement, oak casks

Distilled mainly from corn in traditional Coffey (column) stills that Nikka brought from Scotland in the 1960s, now housed at its Miyagikyo distillery. It shows how rich and flavourful grain whisky can be.

Serve: Neat, on ice or in a highball
