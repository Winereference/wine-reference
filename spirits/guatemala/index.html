Guatemala: Ron de Guatemala

Rum & cane · Guatemala

Guatemalan rum is made from concentrated fresh cane juice and aged slowly in the cool highlands, giving the soft, sweet style of Zacapa and Botran.

SpiritRon de Guatemala
Made fromVirgin sugar cane honey (concentrated first-press juice)
TasteSweet and plush: caramel, honey, raisin, cocoa, vanilla and gentle spice.
How to drinkNeat, over a large ice cube, or in a rich Old Fashioned.

How it's made

Cane honey is fermented and column-distilled on the coast, then the spirit is taken up to the highlands around Quetzaltenango to age in a solera-style system of casks that once held whiskey, sherry and wine.

Rum is fermented from virgin sugar cane honey, the first-press juice concentrated by gentle heating, then distilled in column stills near the cane fields on the Pacific coast.

History

Guatemala grew cane from colonial times. The Botran family began making rum in Quetzaltenango in 1939, and Ron Zacapa was created in 1976 for the centenary of the town of Zacapa.

Ageing and label terms

Spirit is aged in the highlands, Zacapa at about 2,300 metres, where cool air slows ageing. A solera-style system moves rum through casks that held American whiskey, sherry and Pedro Ximénez. Age statements show the oldest rum in the blend.

Styles

Mostly rich, sweet sipping rums, from young añejos to long solera blends.

Rules and protected names

Ron de Guatemala is a protected geographical indication: rum must be made in Guatemala from local sugar cane.

How to serve and classic cocktails

Best neat, on ice or in a spirit-forward Old Fashioned or Manhattan twist.

With food

Pairs with dark chocolate, caramel desserts, coffee, roast pork and mild cigars.

Good to know

The highland ageing gives Guatemalan rum its trademark soft sweetness, though some critics note the style can taste very sweet.

Producers

  • Ron Zacapa
  • Botran
  • Ron Quetzalteca
