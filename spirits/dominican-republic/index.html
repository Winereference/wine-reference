Dominican Republic: Dominican rum (ron dominicano)

Rum & cane · Dominican Republic

The Dominican Republic makes light, dry, Spanish-style rum and is one of the largest rum exporters in the Caribbean, led by big family houses such as Brugal and Barceló.

SpiritDominican rum (ron dominicano)
Made fromSugar cane molasses
TasteLight to medium bodied and often quite dry, with vanilla, oak, toffee and dried fruit in older blends.
How to drinkNeat or on ice for older blends; white and young rums with cola, lime and soda or in a Daiquiri.

How it's made

Molasses is fermented and distilled in column stills to a high strength for a light, clean spirit. By local rules rum must age at least one year in oak, and most houses use ex-bourbon barrels, often finished or blended through sherry casks.

Most Dominican rum is made from molasses, fermented for a short time and distilled in multi-column stills to a high strength, giving a light, clean spirit. Houses then shape flavour through cask choice and blending rather than heavy distillates.

History

Cane came to Hispaniola with Columbus, but a modern rum industry grew from the late 1800s, when Andrés Brugal founded his company in Puerto Plata in 1888 and Julián Barceló started his in Santo Domingo in 1929. Today rum is a national drink and a major export.

Ageing and label terms

Rum must age at least one year in oak barrels. Labels such as Añejo, Extra Viejo and Gran Reserva are used by producers, and age statements show the youngest rum in the blend. Ex-bourbon American oak is standard, with sherry casks used for finishing.

Styles

Light white rums (blanco) for mixing, golden añejos, and dry, oaky extra viejo and gran reserva blends for sipping. Some brands add sweetness, while Brugal is known for a very dry style.

Rules and protected names

Ron de República Dominicana is a protected geographical indication (since 2013), requiring distillation, ageing and bottling in the country and at least one year in oak.

How to serve and classic cocktails

Sip older rums neat or over a large ice cube. Younger rums suit a Cuba Libre, a Daiquiri, a Mojito or a simple rum and ginger.

With food

Older dry rums go well with cigars, dark chocolate, roasted nuts and caramel desserts.

Good to know

Brugal is still run by descendants of the founding family, and its Puerto Plata ageing cellars are open to visitors.

Producers

  • Brugal
  • Barceló
  • Bermúdez
  • Ron Atlántico

Bottles to try

Rum & cane · Dominican Republic

Brugal 1888

Dry and elegant, with toffee, dried fig, vanilla and a long, oaky finish.

Strength: 40% · Age and cask: Double aged, ex-bourbon American oak then ex-sherry Spanish oak

Named after the year Andrés Brugal founded the company, this Gran Reserva is first aged in ex-bourbon barrels and then rested in sherry-seasoned casks, showing the dry Brugal house style at its best.

Serve: Neat or over one large ice cube

Rum & cane · Dominican Republic

Brugal Extra Viejo

Light and dry, with oak, cocoa, orange peel and a little spice.

Strength: 38% · Age and cask: Aged blend, American oak

The best-known Brugal sipping rum, a blend of rums aged in ex-bourbon barrels, made in Puerto Plata on the north coast of the Dominican Republic.

Serve: On the rocks or in a rum Old Fashioned

Rum & cane · Dominican Republic

Barceló Imperial

Round and smooth, with vanilla, honey, toasted nuts and dried fruit.

Strength: 38% · Age and cask: Blend of rums aged in American oak

The flagship premium rum of Ron Barceló, a Santo Domingo house founded in 1929, made from cane juice distillate and aged in ex-bourbon barrels; one of the most widely sold Dominican sipping rums.

Serve: Neat or on ice with an orange twist

Rum & cane · Dominican Republic

Ron Barceló Gran Añejo

Smooth and sweet-leaning, with vanilla, toffee, dried fruit and a gentle oak spice.

Age and cask: Aged in ex-bourbon American oak

Barceló distils from fresh sugar cane juice rather than molasses in the Dominican Republic; Gran Añejo is its widely sold aged blend and a good-value sipping rum.

Serve: On the rocks or with cola and lime

Rum & cane · Dominican Republic

Brugal Añejo

Light and dry for an aged rum, with oak, cocoa, orange peel and a short, clean finish.

Strength: 38% · Age and cask: Aged in American oak

Brugal is the best-known rum house of the Dominican Republic, founded in Puerto Plata in 1888, and is known for a deliberately dry style with little or no added sugar.

Serve: Long with ginger ale or in a Cuba Libre

Rum & cane · Dominican Republic

Ron Barceló Añejo

Light and smooth, with vanilla, caramel, a little banana and gentle oak.

Age and cask: Aged in ex-bourbon oak barrels

Barceló distils from fresh sugar cane juice rather than molasses, which is unusual for a Dominican rum. Founded by Julián Barceló in Santo Domingo in 1929, it is one of the best-selling rums in the Spanish-speaking world.

Serve: With cola and lime, or over ice

Rum & cane · Dominican Republic

Brugal Leyenda Selección Homenaje

Dry and polished, with dried fruit, cocoa, sweet spice and toasted oak.

Strength: 38%

A tribute to the Brugal family founders, made in the house style of light, dry rums with no added sugar. It sits between the everyday Añejo and the prestige 1888 in the range.

Serve: Neat or with one large ice cube
