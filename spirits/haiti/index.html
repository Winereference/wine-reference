Haiti: Rhum and clairin

Rum & cane · Haiti

Haiti has hundreds of small village distilleries making clairin, an unaged cane spirit, and one famous rum house, Barbancourt, founded in 1862.

SpiritRhum and clairin
Made fromFresh sugar cane juice (some molasses)
TasteClairin: wild, grassy, briny and fruity. Aged rhum: dried fruit, vanilla, cane and soft spice.
How to drinkClairin in a Ti' Punch or daiquiri; aged Barbancourt neat or over ice.

How it's made

Clairin is made from fresh cane juice or syrup, fermented with wild yeasts and distilled once in small pot stills, usually bottled at full strength. Barbancourt double-distils cane juice and ages it in Limousin oak.

Clairin makers crush local heirloom cane, often grown without chemicals, and ferment the juice or boiled syrup with wild yeasts for several days. It is distilled once in small pot or simple column stills and bottled at full strength. Barbancourt ferments fresh cane juice and double-distils it in columns and copper pot stills.

History

Sugar cane came to Haiti in colonial times and the island was once the world's largest sugar producer. Dupré Barbancourt founded his rum house in 1862 using Cognac methods, and clairin has long been made in hundreds of rural distilleries for local drinking and Vodou ceremonies.

Ageing and label terms

Clairin is nearly always unaged. Barbancourt ages in Limousin oak: the 3 Star is 4 years, the 5 Star Réserve Spéciale 8 years and the Réserve du Domaine 15 years.

Styles

Raw, wild clairin blanc and smooth, aged rhum. Some clairin is infused with herbs, bark and spices for local use.

Rules and protected names

Haiti has no formal appellation for rum. Clairin is defined by tradition: fresh cane, wild fermentation, no additives.

How to serve and classic cocktails

Clairin shines in a Ti' Punch, daiquiri or with tropical juices. Aged Barbancourt is good neat, on ice or in an Old Fashioned.

With food

Pairs with griot (fried pork), accra fritters, grilled fish, spicy creole sauces and coconut desserts.

Good to know

Velier's Spirit of Haiti project, started in 2014, brought single-village clairins such as Sajous, Vaval and Casimir to drinkers worldwide.

Bottles to try

Rum & cane · Haiti

Rhum Barbancourt Réserve Spéciale 5 Étoiles 8 Ans

Dried fruit, vanilla, toasted oak and fresh cane; dry, elegant and smooth.

Strength: 43% · Age and cask: 8 years, Limousin oak

Barbancourt was founded in Port-au-Prince in 1862 by Dupré Barbancourt, who brought Cognac methods to Haiti. It is made from fresh cane juice, double-distilled, and still family-run.

Serve: Neat or on a large ice cube

Rum & cane · Haiti

Rhum Barbancourt 3 Étoiles 4 Ans

Fresh cane, banana and light vanilla with a crisp, dry finish.

Strength: 43% · Age and cask: 4 years, oak

The everyday aged rhum from Haiti's oldest rum house, distilled from fresh cane juice. A good-value classic that works neat or in cocktails.

Serve: In a daiquiri or rum and ginger

Rum & cane · Haiti

Clairin Sajous Clairin

Wild and vivid: cut grass, olive brine, ripe fruit and pepper.

Age and cask: Unaged

Made by Michel Sajous at Chelo distillery in Saint-Michel-de-l'Attalaye from organic Cristalline cane, fermented with wild yeasts and distilled once. Each vintage is bottled at full strength, so the ABV varies.

Serve: In a Ti' Punch with lime and cane syrup

Rum & cane · Haiti

Clairin Vaval Clairin

Wild and grassy: green cane, brine, citrus and tropical fruit.

Age and cask: Unaged

Made by Fritz Vaval at Cavaillon in southern Haiti from fresh cane juice with wild fermentation and a single pot-still distillation, bottled at full strength.

Serve: In a Ti' Punch or daiquiri
