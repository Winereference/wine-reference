Brazil: Cachaça

Rum & cane · Brazil

Brazil's national spirit, made all over the country, with famous craft cachaça from Salinas in Minas Gerais.

SpiritCachaça
Made fromFresh sugar cane juice
TasteFresh cane, grass and banana; wood-aged styles add spice and cinnamon.
How to drinkIn a Caipirinha with lime and sugar.

How it's made

Distilled from fermented cane juice to 38–48% ABV. It may be unaged or aged in native woods such as amburana as well as oak.

Freshly pressed sugar cane juice is fermented, in small farms often with wild yeasts and a cornmeal starter, then distilled. Craft cachaça is usually made in copper pot stills (alambiques), while big brands use column stills.

History

Cachaça was first made on the sugar plantations of colonial Brazil in the 1500s, which makes it one of the oldest spirits in the Americas. It went from a drink of plantation workers to a national symbol, and Brazil now protects the name by law.

Ageing and label terms

Unaged cachaça is called prata, branca or clássica. Aged cachaça can rest in oak or in native Brazilian woods such as amburana, jequitibá and bálsamo, which give very different spicy, nutty or floral notes; 'envelhecida', 'premium' and 'extra premium' mark longer ageing.

Styles

Clear, grassy cachaças for mixing, gentle ones rested in neutral wood, and amber cachaças from native woods or oak for sipping.

Rules and protected names

Cachaça must be made in Brazil from sugar cane juice and bottled between 38% and 48% abv. Small amounts of sugar are allowed; above that limit the label must say 'adoçada' (sweetened).

How to serve and classic cocktails

The Caipirinha with lime and sugar is Brazil's national cocktail. Batidas with fruit and condensed milk, and simply cachaça with tonic, are also popular.

With food

Feijoada, pão de queijo, fried snacks like coxinha and grilled meats from a churrasco.

Good to know

For years the US called cachaça 'Brazilian rum'; it has been recognised there as a distinct product of Brazil since 2013.

Bottles to try

Rum & cane · Brazil

Leblon Cachaça

Grassy fresh cane, green banana, citrus and a light vanilla note.

Strength: 40% · Age and cask: Rested briefly in French oak casks

Distilled in copper pot stills in Minas Gerais from fresh sugar-cane juice, not molasses, then rested for a short time in casks before bottling.

Serve: In a Caipirinha

Rum & cane · Brazil

Novo Fogo Silver Cachaça

Fresh and fruity: banana, green cane, citrus and a light peppery finish.

Strength: 40% · Age and cask: Unaged, rested in stainless steel

An organic cachaça made in Morretes, in the Brazilian state of Paraná, between the coastal rainforest and the mountains. The cane is hand-cut and the fresh juice is distilled in copper pot stills.

Serve: In a Caipirinha

Rum & cane · Brazil

Avuá Amburana

Cinnamon, clove and marzipan over fresh grassy cane; soft and exotic.

Strength: 40% · Age and cask: Aged in amburana wood

Cachaça from fresh-pressed sugar cane, pot-distilled in Rio de Janeiro state, then aged in barrels of amburana, a native Brazilian wood that gives spicy, sweet aromas.

Serve: Neat, or in an Old Fashioned with a dash of bitters

Rum & cane · Brazil

Cachaça 51 Pirassununga 51

Grassy, fresh cane with a slightly sweet, peppery bite.

Age and cask: Unaged

Made by Companhia Müller de Bebidas in Pirassununga, São Paulo state, it is the best-selling cachaça in Brazil. Distilled from fresh fermented sugar cane juice, it is the everyday caipirinha base.

Serve: In a caipirinha with lime and sugar

Rum & cane · Brazil

Avuá Prata

Bright and grassy: fresh cane, banana, pepper and citrus.

Strength: 42% · Age and cask: Unaged, rested in steel

Made from fresh cane juice at a family farm in Carmo near Rio de Janeiro, fermented with wild yeasts and distilled in a copper pot still.

Serve: In a caipirinha
