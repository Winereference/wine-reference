Tannat grape

Photo: Guillermo Vuljevas on Unsplash

Red grape

Tannat is a red grape known for blackberry, plum, spice. Its best-known homes are Uruguay, Madiran.

Primary flavours

  • 🫐Blackberry
  • 🫐Plum
  • 🫚Spice
  • 🫐Black plum

Sweetness

Tannin

Key regionsUruguay, Madiran

Origin and history

From south-west France, around Madiran and the Basque foothills; its name probably refers to its high tannin.

Where it grows

Madiran, Saint-Mont, Tursan and Irouléguy in south-west France, Uruguay (Canelones and Maldonado), Argentina, Brazil and Virginia.

Structure

Full body, very deep colour, high acidity and very high, firm tannins; Uruguayan versions are often softer.

Aromas, young and aged

Young: blackberry, black plum, black currant, liquorice and spice. With age: leather, tobacco, earth and dried fruit.

Ageing

Most need 3–5 years; top Madiran can age 20 years or more.

Also known as

Harriague (Uruguay).

Good to know

Basque immigrant Pascual Harriague brought it to Uruguay in 1870, where it is now the national grape; micro-oxygenation was invented in Madiran in 1990 to soften it.

Food to pair with Tannat

  • Cassoulet: Firm, tannic South-West French reds cut through duck fat, sausage and beans, the same pairing locals drink in Toulouse and Castelnaudary.
  • Duck confit: South-west France's tannic Cahors and Madiran reds are the local match, cutting through the rich, salty, crisp-skinned duck.
