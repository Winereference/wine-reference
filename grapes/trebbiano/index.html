Trebbiano grape

White grape

Trebbiano is a white grape known for lemon, apple, almond. Its best-known homes are Italy.

StyleWhite
Body●●○○○ (2/5)
FlavoursLemon, apple, almond
Key regionsItaly

Origin and history

A group of Italian varieties; the most widespread, Trebbiano Toscano, probably originated in central Italy.

Where it grows

Throughout Italy (Tuscany, Emilia-Romagna, Abruzzo, Umbria), and as Ugni Blanc in Cognac, Armagnac, Gascony, Argentina and Australia.

Structure

Light to medium body with high acidity and modest alcohol; usually dry and neutral, also sweet in Vin Santo.

Aromas, young and aged

Young: lemon, green apple, almond and white flowers. With age (rare, top examples): honey, nuts and herbs.

Ageing

Most is best young, within 1–2 years; Valentini's Trebbiano d'Abruzzo can age 15–20+ years.

Also known as

Ugni Blanc (France), Saint-Émilion (Cognac), Trebbiano Toscano.

Good to know

As Ugni Blanc it is the main grape distilled into Cognac and Armagnac, because its high acidity and low alcohol suit distillation.
