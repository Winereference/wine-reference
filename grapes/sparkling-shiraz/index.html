Sparkling Shiraz

Sparkling

Sparkling Shiraz is a sparkling wine style known for blackberry, plum, spice. Its best-known homes are Australia.

StyleSparkling
Body●●●●○ (4/5)
FlavoursBlackberry, plum, spice
Key regionsAustralia
Food pairingRoast duck, barbecue

Origin and history

An Australian speciality dating to the 1880s, when Edmund Mazure made 'Sparkling Burgundy' in Adelaide; Seppelt at Great Western in Victoria later made it a tradition.

Where it grows

Australia: Great Western (Grampians) in Victoria, the Barossa Valley, McLaren Vale and other South Australian regions.

Structure

Medium to full body, soft tannins, fresh acidity and frothy deep-red mousse; usually off-dry, with a dosage sometimes including old Shiraz or fortified wine.

Aromas, young and aged

Young: blackberry, plum, black cherry, spice and chocolate. With age: leather, earth, savoury spice and dried fruit.

Ageing

Most within 2–5 years; top examples such as Seppelt Show Sparkling Shiraz can age 10–20 years or more.

Also known as

Sparkling Red, formerly Sparkling Burgundy.

Good to know

A traditional Australian Christmas wine; Rockford's Black Shiraz from the Barossa is a cult bottle, and most quality examples are made by the traditional method.
