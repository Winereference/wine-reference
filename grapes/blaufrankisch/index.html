Blaufränkisch grape

Red grape

Blaufränkisch is a red grape known for blackberry, cherry, pepper. Its best-known homes are Burgenland.

StyleRed
Body●●●○○ (3/5)
FlavoursBlackberry, cherry, pepper
Key regionsBurgenland

Origin and history

Central European, with roots in the old Austro-Hungarian lands; the 'fränkisch' tag was a medieval mark of quality grapes rather than a link to Franconia.

Where it grows

Burgenland in Austria (Mittelburgenland, Leithaberg, Eisenberg), Sopron, Eger and Szekszárd in Hungary, Württemberg in Germany, plus Slovenia and Croatia.

Structure

Medium to full body, bright high acidity and firm, fine-grained tannins; cooler sites give a lighter, more mineral style.

Aromas, young and aged

Young: blackberry, dark cherry, black pepper and violet. With age: forest floor, dried cherry, spice and savoury earth.

Ageing

Simple examples drink within 2–5 years; top single-vineyard Burgenland wines age 10–20 years.

Also known as

Lemberger (Germany), Kékfrankos (Hungary), Frankovka (Czechia, Slovakia, Croatia), Modra Frankinja (Slovenia).

Good to know

It is a parent of Zweigelt, Austria's most planted red, which was bred from Blaufränkisch and St. Laurent in 1922.
