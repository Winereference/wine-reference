St. Laurent grape

Red grape

St. Laurent is a red grape known for cherry, plum, spice. Its best-known homes are Austria.

StyleRed
Body●●○○○ (2/5)
FlavoursCherry, plum, spice
Key regionsAustria

Origin and history

An old Austrian variety, possibly a Pinot Noir seedling though this is unproven; it is named after St Lawrence, whose feast day (10 August) is when its grapes begin to change colour.

Where it grows

Austria's Thermenregion and Burgenland (Neusiedlersee), Czechia's Moravia, Slovakia, and Pfalz in Germany.

Structure

Light to medium body, soft, silky tannins and fresh acidity, often compared to Pinot Noir but with deeper colour.

Aromas, young and aged

Young: sour cherry, plum, blackberry and a hint of spice. With age: forest floor, dried cherry, smoke and earth.

Ageing

Most drink within 2–6 years; top Thermenregion and Burgenland examples can age 10–15 years.

Also known as

Sankt Laurent, Svatovavřinecké (Czechia), Svätovavrinecké (Slovakia).

Good to know

Tricky to grow, prone to coulure and rot, it nearly vanished; together with Blaufränkisch it is a parent of Zweigelt.
