Lambrusco grape

Red grape

Lambrusco is a red grape known for red cherry, strawberry, violet. Its best-known homes are Emilia-Romagna.

StyleRed
Body●●○○○ (2/5)
FlavoursRed cherry, strawberry, violet
Key regionsEmilia-Romagna

Origin and history

Not one grape but a family of distinct native varieties of Emilia-Romagna and Lombardy, named from the Latin 'labrusca', meaning wild vine.

Where it grows

Emilia-Romagna around Modena and Reggio Emilia (Lambrusco di Sorbara, Grasparossa di Castelvetro, Salamino di Santa Croce, Reggiano) and Lombardy (Lambrusco Mantovano).

Structure

Light to medium body, frizzante (gently sparkling), high acidity and low to medium tannin; ranges from bone dry (secco) to sweet (dolce or amabile).

Aromas, young and aged

Young: red cherry, strawberry, raspberry, violet and rhubarb; Sorbara is pale and floral, Grasparossa dark and tannic. Not made for ageing.

Ageing

Drink within 1–3 years of the vintage.

Also known as

Varieties include Lambrusco di Sorbara, Grasparossa, Salamino, Maestri, Marani and Viadanese.

Good to know

Sweet Lambrusco was one of the biggest-selling imported wines in the US in the 1970s and 1980s; dry versions are the classic match for Modena's salumi.
