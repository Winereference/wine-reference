Cinsault grape

Red grape

Cinsault is a red grape known for red cherry, strawberry, floral. Its best-known homes are Languedoc, South Africa.

StyleRed
Body●●○○○ (2/5)
FlavoursRed cherry, strawberry, floral
Key regionsLanguedoc, South Africa

Origin and history

An old variety of southern France, probably from Provence or the Languedoc, valued for heat and drought tolerance.

Where it grows

Languedoc and Provence (especially rosé), the southern Rhône, South Africa, Lebanon's Bekaa Valley, Morocco and Puglia.

Structure

Light to medium body, pale colour, moderate acidity and low, soft tannins.

Aromas, young and aged

Young: red cherry, strawberry, raspberry and floral, perfumed notes. With age (old-vine examples): dried cherry, earth and spice.

Ageing

Most best within 1–4 years; old-vine South African and Lebanese reds can age 10.

Also known as

Cinsaut, Hermitage (historic South African name), Ottavianello (Puglia).

Good to know

Crossed with Pinot Noir in 1925 by Abraham Perold, it gave South Africa Pinotage; it is also a key part of Lebanon's Château Musar.
