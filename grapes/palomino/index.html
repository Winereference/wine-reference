Palomino grape

Photo: inma santiago on Unsplash

White grape

Palomino is a white grape known for almond, apple, saline. Its best-known homes are Jerez.

Primary flavours

  • 🌰Almond
  • 🍏Apple
  • 🌊Saline
  • 🍞Bread dough

Key regionsJerez

Origin and history

An Andalusian grape, grown around Jerez since at least the Middle Ages; legend links its name to a 13th-century knight, Fernán Yáñez Palomino.

Where it grows

Jerez, Sanlúcar de Barrameda and El Puerto de Santa María in Andalucía; also Galicia, the Canary Islands (as Listán Blanco), South Africa and Australia.

Structure

Medium to full body, low acidity and dry; neutral grapes that become fino, manzanilla, amontillado and oloroso sherry.

Aromas, young and aged

Fino/manzanilla: almond, apple, saline and bread dough. With age (amontillado/oloroso): walnut, toffee, orange peel and spice.

Ageing

Fino and manzanilla are best fresh after bottling; long-aged amontillado and oloroso can last decades.

Also known as

Palomino Fino, Listán Blanco (Canary Islands).

Good to know

Grown on Jerez's white albariza chalk soils, it is the grape behind nearly all dry sherry, aged in the solera system under flor.

Food to pair with Palomino

  • Patatas bravas: A tapas-bar classic with tapas-bar wines: chilled fino or a fruity Garnacha refreshes against crisp potatoes and smoky paprika sauce.
  • Gambas al ajillo: Garlic, chilli and olive oil call for bone-dry, tangy Spanish whites; fino sherry's salty bite is the classic tapas-bar match.
  • Gazpacho: Cold tomato soup with vinegar is hard on most wines, but tangy fino sherry or a zesty Spanish white matches its acidity.
  • Cheese straws: Crisp, salty cheese pastries are made for sparkling wine or bone-dry fino sherry, whose acidity and tang refresh the palate.
  • Spanish tortilla: Tapas-bar classics: salty fino or fresh Albariño refresh the eggy potato, while a soft young Rioja suits it as a supper.
