Mourvèdre grape

Red grape

Mourvèdre is a red grape known for blackberry, plum, pepper, earth. Its best-known homes are Bandol, Spain.

StyleRed
Body●●●●● (5/5)
FlavoursBlackberry, plum, pepper, earth
Key regionsBandol, Spain

Origin and history

From Spain, where it is called Monastrell; the names Mourvèdre and Mataró probably derive from Murviedro (Sagunto) near Valencia and Mataró near Barcelona.

Where it grows

Bandol and the southern Rhône in France, south-east Spain (Jumilla, Yecla, Alicante, Bullas), and Barossa and McLaren Vale in Australia, plus California.

Structure

Full body, deep colour, moderate acidity and high, firm tannins with fairly high alcohol.

Aromas, young and aged

Young: blackberry, black plum, black pepper, violet and dried herbs. With age: game, leather, earth, tobacco and meat.

Ageing

Most best within 3–8 years; top Bandol needs 10 years and can age 20 or more.

Also known as

Monastrell (Spain), Mataró (Australia, California).

Good to know

Red Bandol must contain at least 50% Mourvèdre and spend at least 18 months in wood; the grape needs heat and likes to 'see the sea'.
