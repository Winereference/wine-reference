Kerner grape

White grape

Kerner is a white grape known for peach, apple, spice. Its best-known homes are Alto Adige.

StyleWhite
Body●●●○○ (3/5)
FlavoursPeach, apple, spice
Key regionsAlto Adige

Origin and history

Bred in 1929 at Weinsberg in Württemberg by August Herold, crossing Trollinger (Schiava Grossa) with Riesling; named after the poet Justinus Kerner.

Where it grows

Germany (Rheinhessen, Pfalz, Württemberg) and Italy's Alto Adige, notably the Eisacktal (Valle Isarco); small plantings in Switzerland and Japan.

Structure

Light to medium body with fresh, Riesling-like acidity; made dry to off-dry, occasionally as sweet late-harvest wine.

Aromas, young and aged

Young: green apple, peach, lime and a light spicy, grassy note. With age: honey, quince and subtle petrol-like hints.

Ageing

Most is best within 2–5 years; the best Eisacktal examples can age for 8–10.

Good to know

Its frost resistance makes it ideal for high, cool sites; Abbazia di Novacella in the Eisacktal is one of its best-known producers.
