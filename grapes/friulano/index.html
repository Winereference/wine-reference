Friulano grape

White grape

Friulano is a white grape known for pear, almond, herbs. Its best-known homes are Friuli.

StyleWhite
Body●●●○○ (3/5)
FlavoursPear, almond, herbs
Key regionsFriuli

Origin and history

Originally from south-west France; DNA shows it is the same grape as Sauvignonasse (Sauvignon Vert). Long called Tocai Friulano in north-east Italy.

Where it grows

Friuli: Collio, Friuli Colli Orientali and Isonzo; also Slovenian Brda, the Veneto, and Chile, where it was long mistaken for Sauvignon Blanc.

Structure

Medium to full body, moderate acidity and a rich, slightly oily texture; dry, with a signature bitter twist on the finish.

Aromas, young and aged

Young: pear, green apple, almond, meadow flowers and fresh herbs. With age: honey, hazelnut, chamomile and a gently nutty richness.

Ageing

Most is best within 1–4 years; top Collio and Colli Orientali bottles can age for 8–10 years.

Also known as

Sauvignonasse, Sauvignon Vert, formerly Tocai Friulano; Jakot in Slovenia.

Good to know

An EU ruling protecting Hungary's Tokaj banned the name 'Tocai' from 2007; some Friulian growers pointedly labelled their wine 'Jakot', Tokaj spelt backwards.
