Grüner Veltliner grape

White grape

Grüner Veltliner is a white grape known for green apple, pepper, lime. Its best-known homes are Austria.

StyleWhite
Body●●●○○ (3/5)
FlavoursGreen apple, pepper, lime
Key regionsAustria

Origin and history

An Austrian grape; DNA proves it a cross of Savagnin and St Georgen, an old vine found in Burgenland in 2000.

Where it grows

Austria (Wachau, Kamptal, Kremstal, Weinviertel, Wagram), plus the Czech Republic, Slovakia, Hungary, New Zealand and the US.

Structure

Light to full body with crisp, high acidity; dry, usually unoaked, from light quaffers to rich, powerful wines.

Aromas, young and aged

Young: green apple, lime, white pepper, lentil and celery. With age: honey, toast, nuts and smoky, savoury notes.

Ageing

Everyday wines are best within 1–3 years; top Wachau Smaragd and Kamptal wines can age 15–20+ years.

Also known as

Grüner, Weissgipfler, Veltlínské Zelené.

Good to know

Austria's most planted grape (about a third of vineyards); its peppery 'Pfefferl' character comes from rotundone, the same compound as in Syrah.
