Malvasia grape

White grape

Malvasia is a white grape known for peach, floral, almond. Its best-known homes are Italy, Iberia.

StyleWhite
Body●●●○○ (3/5)
FlavoursPeach, floral, almond
Key regionsItaly, Iberia

Origin and history

A family name rather than one grape: it covers many unrelated varieties, named after the Greek port of Monemvasia through which Venetian merchants traded sweet wine.

Where it grows

Italy (Frascati, Lipari, Friuli, Sardinia, Piacenza), Spain (Canaries, Rioja, Sitges), Portugal (Madeira, Douro), Croatia's Istria, Slovenia and Greece.

Structure

Typically medium to full body with low to medium acidity; ranges from dry and textured to sweet, dried-grape and fortified styles.

Aromas, young and aged

Young: apricot, peach, orange blossom, almond and sometimes musky spice. With age: honey, dried fruit, nuts and caramel.

Ageing

Dry wines within 1–4 years; sweet passito and fortified Malmsey Madeira can last many decades.

Also known as

Malvasía, Malmsey (Madeira), Malvazija Istarska (Croatia), Malvoisie.

Good to know

Malmsey, the English name for Malvasia wine, was so prized that legend says the Duke of Clarence was drowned in a butt of it in 1478.
