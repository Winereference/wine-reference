Chardonnay grape

White grape

Chardonnay is a white grape known for apple, lemon, butter, oak. Its best-known homes are Burgundy, California, Australia.

StyleWhite
Body●●●○○ (3/5)
FlavoursApple, lemon, butter, oak
Key regionsBurgundy, California, Australia
Food pairingLobster, roast chicken, cream sauces

Origin and history

From Burgundy; a natural cross of Pinot and the old peasant grape Gouais Blanc. A village called Chardonnay lies in the Mâconnais.

Where it grows

Burgundy (Chablis, Meursault, Puligny-Montrachet), Champagne, California, Australia (Margaret River, Yarra Valley), New Zealand, Chile and South Africa.

Structure

Medium to full body with medium to high acidity. A fairly neutral grape that shows its place and winemaking clearly.

Aromas, young and aged

Cool and unoaked: green apple, lemon and wet stone. Warm: peach, pineapple and melon. Oak and malolactic fermentation add butter, vanilla, toast and hazelnut.

Ageing

Most drink well young; top white Burgundy can age 10–20 years.

Also known as

Morillon (Styria, Austria).

Good to know

The only grape in Blanc de Blancs Champagne and in Chablis.
