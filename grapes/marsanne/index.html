Marsanne grape

White grape

Marsanne is a white grape known for pear, almond, honey. Its best-known homes are Rhône.

StyleWhite
Body●●●●○ (4/5)
FlavoursPear, almond, honey
Key regionsRhône

Origin and history

From the northern Rhône, named after the village of Marsanne near Montélimar.

Where it grows

Northern Rhône (Hermitage, Crozes-Hermitage, Saint-Joseph, Saint-Péray), southern France, Switzerland's Valais, Victoria in Australia and California.

Structure

Full body, low to moderate acidity and a rich, waxy, oily texture; dry, sometimes barrel-aged.

Aromas, young and aged

Young: pear, melon, almond, white flowers and quince. With age: honey, beeswax, marzipan, hazelnut and toast.

Ageing

Often best young or after 7+ years, with a dumb phase in between; white Hermitage can age 20–30 years.

Also known as

Ermitage (Valais), Grosse Roussette.

Good to know

Tahbilk in Victoria has some of the world's oldest Marsanne vines, planted in 1927, and the largest single holding of the grape.
