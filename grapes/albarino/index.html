Albariño grape

White grape

Albariño is a white grape known for peach, citrus, saline. Its best-known homes are Rías Baixas, Vinho Verde.

StyleWhite
Body●●○○○ (2/5)
FlavoursPeach, citrus, saline
Key regionsRías Baixas, Vinho Verde

Origin and history

Native to Galicia in north-west Spain and Portugal's Minho; the story that monks brought it from the Rhine is a myth.

Where it grows

Rías Baixas (Val do Salnés, O Rosal, Condado do Tea) in Spain; Monção e Melgaço in Vinho Verde; also Uruguay, California and New Zealand.

Structure

Light to medium body, high acidity, usually dry, with a slightly saline, mouthwatering finish.

Aromas, young and aged

Young: white peach, apricot, lemon, grapefruit and sea salt. With age: honey, nuts and a petrol-like note.

Ageing

Most best within 1–3 years; top Rías Baixas and Monção e Melgaço can age 5–10 years.

Also known as

Alvarinho (Portugal), Alvarín Blanco.

Good to know

Rías Baixas vines are often trained on high granite pergolas ('parras') to let the Atlantic breeze dry the grapes and prevent rot.
