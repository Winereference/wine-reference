Carignan grape

Red grape

Carignan is a red grape known for blackberry, cherry, herbs. Its best-known homes are Languedoc, Sardinia.

StyleRed
Body●●●●○ (4/5)
FlavoursBlackberry, cherry, herbs
Key regionsLanguedoc, Sardinia

Origin and history

Originally from Aragon in Spain, named after the town of Cariñena; once the most planted grape in France, largely in the Languedoc.

Where it grows

Languedoc-Roussillon (Corbières, Minervois, Faugères), Spain (Priorat, Montsant, Rioja as Mazuelo), Sardinia (Carignano del Sulcis), Chile's Maule and California.

Structure

Medium to full body, deep colour, high acidity and high, sometimes astringent tannins; old vines give the best balance.

Aromas, young and aged

Young: blackberry, black cherry, red plum, liquorice and dried herbs. With age: dried fruit, leather, earth and savoury spice.

Ageing

Most best within 2–6 years; old-vine examples from Priorat or Sardinia age 10–15.

Also known as

Cariñena, Mazuelo (Rioja), Samsó (Catalonia), Carignano (Sardinia), Carignane (US).

Good to know

In Chile's Maule Valley, VIGNO is a growers' association making dry-farmed, old-vine Carignan, Chile's first producer-led wine appellation.
