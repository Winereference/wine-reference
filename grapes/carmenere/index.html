Carménère grape

Red grape

Carménère is a red grape known for black cherry, pepper, herbs. Its best-known homes are Chile.

StyleRed
Body●●●●○ (4/5)
FlavoursBlack cherry, pepper, herbs
Key regionsChile

Origin and history

An old Bordeaux variety, DNA-shown to be a child of Cabernet Franc and Gros Cabernet; it nearly vanished from Bordeaux after phylloxera.

Where it grows

Chile (Colchagua, Cachapoal and its Peumo area, Maipo), plus north-east Italy and China, where vines called 'Cabernet Gernischt' proved to be Carménère.

Structure

Medium to full body, deep colour, moderate acidity and soft, round tannins.

Aromas, young and aged

Young: black cherry, plum, red pepper, green peppercorn, paprika and herbs. With age: tobacco, leather, cocoa and spice.

Ageing

Most best within 2–6 years; top Chilean examples age 10–15.

Also known as

Grande Vidure (historic Bordeaux), Cabernet Gernischt (China).

Good to know

In Chile it was mistaken for Merlot until 1994, when French ampelographer Jean-Michel Boursiquot identified it.
