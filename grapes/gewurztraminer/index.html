Gewürztraminer grape

Photo: Marco Pregnolato on Unsplash

White grape

Gewürztraminer is a white grape known for lychee, rose, spice. Its best-known homes are Alsace.

StyleWhite
Body●●●●○ (4/5)
FlavoursLychee, rose, spice
Key regionsAlsace

Origin and history

An aromatic, pink-skinned form of the ancient Savagnin (Traminer) family; the Traminer name links it to Tramin (Termeno) in Alto Adige.

Where it grows

Alsace above all, plus Alto Adige, Germany (Pfalz), Austria, New Zealand (Gisborne), Oregon, Washington and Chile.

Structure

Full body, low acidity and often high alcohol; ranges from dry to off-dry to lusciously sweet.

Aromas, young and aged

Young: lychee, rose petal, ginger, Turkish delight and sweet spice. With age: honey, candied fruit, smoke and exotic spice.

Ageing

Dry styles are best within 2–6 years; Alsace Grand Cru, Vendange Tardive and Sélection de Grains Nobles can age 10–20+ years.

Also known as

Traminer Aromatico, Roter Traminer, Gewürztraminer (also spelt Gewurztraminer).

Good to know

'Gewürz' is German for spice; its pink skins mean the wine often has a deep golden colour, and it is a classic match for Munster cheese.

Food to pair with Gewürztraminer

  • Pad Thai: Sweet, sour, salty and spicy flavours suit aromatic whites; Riesling's lime acidity and Gewürztraminer's lychee match tamarind and peanuts.
  • Thai green curry: Coconut, lemongrass and green chilli suit off-dry, aromatic whites whose touch of sweetness tames heat and echoes the herbs.
  • Crispy duck pancakes: Pinot Noir's cherry fruit matches sweet hoisin and rich duck, while spicy Gewürztraminer picks up five-spice and plum.
  • Chicken tikka masala: Aromatic, off-dry whites soothe the spice and echo the creamy, fragrant sauce; high tannins would only amplify the heat.
  • Beef rendang: Rich coconut, lemongrass and slow-cooked beef can take a ripe, fruity red, while spicy Gewürztraminer mirrors the aromatics.
  • Jerk chicken: Scotch bonnet heat and allspice call for aromatic whites with a touch of sweetness, or a juicy low-tannin red.
  • Lamb biryani: Fragrant spices and tender lamb suit soft, fruity Grenache-based reds or a perfumed Gewurztraminer that echoes cardamom and saffron.
  • Glazed ham: Off-dry Riesling balances the salty-sweet glaze, while juicy, low-tannin Pinot Noir echoes its fruit without clashing.
