Chenin Blanc grape

White grape

Chenin Blanc is a white grape known for apple, quince, honey. Its best-known homes are Loire, South Africa.

StyleWhite
Body●●●○○ (3/5)
FlavoursApple, quince, honey
Key regionsLoire, South Africa

Origin and history

From the Loire Valley, recorded in Anjou by the 16th century; DNA shows a close parent-offspring link with Savagnin.

Where it grows

Loire (Vouvray, Montlouis, Savennières, Saumur, Coteaux du Layon, Bonnezeaux), South Africa (Swartland, Stellenbosch), California and Argentina.

Structure

Light to full body with searing, high acidity; made bone-dry, off-dry, lusciously sweet or as traditional-method sparkling.

Aromas, young and aged

Young: green apple, quince, pear, lemon and chamomile. With age: honey, lanolin, beeswax, marzipan and baked apple.

Ageing

Simple wines 2–4 years; top dry Savennières and Vouvray 15–30 years; sweet Quarts de Chaume and Vouvray Moelleux can last a century.

Also known as

Steen (South Africa), Pineau de la Loire.

Good to know

South Africa has more Chenin Blanc than anywhere else, including France; its old bush vines now make some of the country's most prized whites.
