Muscat grape

Photo: Armands Brants on Unsplash

White grape

Muscat is a white grape known for floral, peach, grape. Its best-known homes are Alsace, Italy.

Primary flavours

  • 🌼Floral
  • 🍑Peach
  • 🍇Grape
  • 🍇Grapey

Taste profile

Sweetness
Body
Tannin
Acidity
Alcohol
Key regionsAlsace, Italy

Origin and history

One of the oldest grape families known; Muscat Blanc à Petits Grains is thought to have spread around the Mediterranean from ancient Greece.

Where it grows

Alsace, Piedmont (Asti), southern France, Spain, Portugal (Setúbal), Greece (Samos), Austria and Rutherglen in Australia.

Structure

Light to full body depending on style, moderate acidity; made dry, gently sparkling, sweet or fortified.

Aromas, young and aged

Young: grapey, orange blossom, peach, rose and musk. With age (sweet/fortified): raisin, toffee, marmalade and coffee.

Ageing

Dry and sparkling styles are best young (1–3 years); fortified Rutherglen Muscat and sweet Samos can last decades.

Also known as

Moscato, Moscatel, Muskateller, Muscat Blanc à Petits Grains, Muscat of Alexandria, Muscat Ottonel.

Good to know

It is one of very few grapes whose wine actually smells of fresh grapes; Moscato d'Asti and Muscat de Beaumes-de-Venise are famous examples.

Food to pair with Muscat

  • Tiramisu: Light, sweet, low-alcohol Moscato d'Asti refreshes after mascarpone, while raisiny Pedro Ximénez sherry echoes the coffee and cocoa.
  • Crème brûlée: Sauternes and botrytised Sémillon bring honey, apricot and acidity that match vanilla custard and caramelised sugar.
  • Sticky toffee pudding: Dates and toffee sauce need an even sweeter wine; PX sherry, Rutherglen Muscat or Tokaji Aszú match the caramel and dried-fruit notes.
  • Lemon tart: A tangy lemon curd tart needs a sweet wine with high acidity, otherwise the dessert makes the wine taste flat.
  • Bakewell tart: Honeyed sweet wines echo the frangipane's almond and the jam's fruit, and must be at least as sweet as the tart.
  • Tarte Tatin: Caramelised apple needs a dessert wine at least as sweet, with acidity to match; Loire Chenin and Barsac both echo baked apple and honey.
  • Christmas pudding: Dried fruit, spice and brandy demand an intensely sweet, nutty wine; Malmsey Madeira, PX sherry or Rutherglen Muscat match its richness.
  • New York cheesecake: Rich, tangy cream cheese needs a sweet wine with lively acidity, such as Moscato d'Asti or a Mosel Auslese.
