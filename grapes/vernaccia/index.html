Vernaccia grape

Photo: Flo P on Unsplash

White grape

Vernaccia is a white grape known for lemon, almond, mineral. Its best-known homes are San Gimignano.

StyleWhite
Body●●○○○ (2/5)
FlavoursLemon, almond, mineral
Key regionsSan Gimignano

Origin and history

Here meaning Vernaccia di San Gimignano, a white grape first documented in the town's archives in 1276; it is unrelated to the other Italian grapes that share the Vernaccia name, which probably comes from the Latin for local or vernacular.

Where it grows

The hills around San Gimignano in Tuscany, where roughly 700 hectares make Vernaccia di San Gimignano, Italy's first ever DOC in 1966 and a DOCG since 1993.

Structure

Light to medium-bodied and dry, with crisp acidity, moderate alcohol and a characteristic slightly bitter, almond-tinged finish.

Aromas, young and aged

Young: lemon, grapefruit, white flowers and a saline, mineral touch. With age: deeper golden colour, honey, toasted almond and hints of flint.

Ageing

Most bottles are best within three years, but top examples and the Riserva can develop well for eight to ten years.

Good to know

Dante placed Pope Martin IV in Purgatory for gluttony, recalling his habit of eating eels drowned in Vernaccia, and Michelangelo the Younger praised the wine in verse.
