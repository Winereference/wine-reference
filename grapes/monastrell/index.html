Monastrell grape

Red grape

Monastrell is a red grape known for blackberry, plum, pepper. Its best-known homes are Jumilla.

StyleRed
Body●●●●● (5/5)
FlavoursBlackberry, plum, pepper
Key regionsJumilla

Origin and history

Spanish in origin, probably from the Valencia region; it spread to France as Mourvèdre, possibly named after Murviedro (modern Sagunto).

Where it grows

Jumilla, Yecla, Alicante and Bullas in south-east Spain; Bandol and the southern Rhône in France; Barossa and McLaren Vale in Australia.

Structure

Full body, high tannin, moderate acidity and often high alcohol; it needs plenty of sun and heat to ripen.

Aromas, young and aged

Young: blackberry, plum, black pepper and violets. With age: game, leather, earth, smoked meat and dried herbs.

Ageing

Everyday Jumilla drinks within 2–5 years; Bandol and top Monastrell can age 10–20 years.

Also known as

Mourvèdre (France), Mataró (Australia, Portugal, Catalonia).

Good to know

Bandol in Provence must contain at least 50% Mourvèdre, making it the grape's most famous French home; it is the M in GSM blends.
