Viognier grape

White grape

Viognier is a white grape known for peach, apricot, honeysuckle. Its best-known homes are Rhône.

StyleWhite
Body●●●●○ (4/5)
FlavoursPeach, apricot, honeysuckle
Key regionsRhône

Origin and history

An ancient grape of the northern Rhône; by the late 1960s only around 14 hectares survived, mostly in Condrieu.

Where it grows

Northern Rhône (Condrieu, Château-Grillet), Languedoc, California, Virginia, Australia (Eden Valley, Barossa), South Africa and Chile.

Structure

Full body, low acidity and often high alcohol; usually dry with an oily, viscous texture.

Aromas, young and aged

Young: apricot, peach, honeysuckle, violet and ginger. With age: honey, dried apricot and spice, though it can lose its perfume.

Ageing

Best young, usually within 1–4 years; a few top Condrieu and Château-Grillet wines last 8–10 years.

Good to know

In Côte-Rôtie up to 20% Viognier may be co-fermented with Syrah, lifting the red's aroma and fixing its colour.
