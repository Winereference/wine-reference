Sparkling

English sparkling wine is a sparkling wine style known for green apple, citrus, brioche. Its best-known homes are Kent, Sussex, Hampshire.

StyleSparkling
Body●●○○○ (2/5)
FlavoursGreen apple, citrus, brioche
Key regionsKent, Sussex, Hampshire
Food pairingOysters, fish and chips

Origin and history

The modern industry took off after Nyetimber planted Chardonnay and the Pinots in West Sussex in 1988; Christopher Merret described adding sugar to make wine sparkle in 1662.

Where it grows

Southern England, especially Kent, Sussex, Hampshire, Surrey and Essex, often on chalk and greensand; Sussex has its own PDO.

Structure

Light to medium body, high, racy acidity and fine bubbles; mostly Brut, made largely by the traditional method from Chardonnay, Pinot Noir and Meunier.

Aromas, young and aged

Young: green apple, lemon, redcurrant and hedgerow fruit. With age: brioche, toast, honey and baked apple.

Ageing

Non-vintage within 2–5 years; top vintage cuvées can age 10 years or more.

Good to know

Champagne houses have invested here: Taittinger planted Domaine Evremond in Kent and Pommery partnered with Hattingley Valley in Hampshire.
