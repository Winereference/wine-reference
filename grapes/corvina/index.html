Corvina grape

Red grape

Corvina is a red grape known for sour cherry, almond, spice. Its best-known homes are Veneto.

StyleRed
Body●●●○○ (3/5)
FlavoursSour cherry, almond, spice
Key regionsVeneto

Origin and history

Native grape of the Veronese hills, the backbone of Valpolicella; DNA shows it is a parent of Rondinella, its usual blending partner.

Where it grows

Veneto: Valpolicella, Amarone della Valpolicella, Recioto della Valpolicella and Bardolino; small plantings in Argentina and Australia.

Structure

Light to medium body, high acidity and low to medium tannin; Amarone, from dried grapes, becomes full-bodied and around 15% alcohol.

Aromas, young and aged

Young: sour cherry, red cherry, almond and herbs. With age, or when dried for Amarone: dried cherry, raisin, chocolate, coffee and spice.

Ageing

Valpolicella is best within 1–4 years; Amarone can age 15–30 years.

Also known as

Corvina Veronese, Cruina.

Good to know

For Amarone the grapes are dried for around three to four months (appassimento) before pressing; Ripasso is Valpolicella refermented on the leftover Amarone skins.
