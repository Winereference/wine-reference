About South West France

Cahors Malbec, Madiran Tannat, sweet Jurançon and a patchwork of local grapes between Bordeaux and the Pyrenees. Mostly Atlantic-influenced, growing warmer and drier inland towards Toulouse, with mild autumns that suit late-picked sweet wines. Cahors spreads over the Lot river terraces and the limestone causse above them; Madiran sits on clay-limestone and gravel; Jurançon's steep slopes in the Pyrenean foothills catch the warm, dry foehn wind in autumn.

South West France: key grapes Malbec, Tannat, Petit Manseng, Gros Manseng, Colombard.

The grapes

  • Tannat (red): blackberry, plum, spice. Best known in Uruguay, Madiran.
  • Cabernet Sauvignon (red): blackcurrant, cedar, graphite. Best known in Bordeaux, Napa Valley, Coonawarra.

More food ideas

  • Steak: Firm tannins in Cabernet and Tempranillo cut through the fat and char of a seared steak, while peppery Shiraz echoes the sauce.
  • Roast leg of lamb: Lamb's rich fat and rosemary call for structured reds; Cabernet's cassis and cedar and Rioja's savoury oak are the classic partners.
  • Beef Wellington: Tender fillet and mushroom duxelles suit a structured but polished red; Bordeaux or Burgundy matches the richness without overpowering the pastry.
  • Cassoulet: Firm, tannic South-West French reds cut through duck fat, sausage and beans, the same pairing locals drink in Toulouse and Castelnaudary.
  • Steak and ale pie: Structured, savoury reds stand up to slow-braised beef and the malty depth of an ale gravy under buttery pastry.
