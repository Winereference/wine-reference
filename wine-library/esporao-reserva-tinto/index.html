About Alentejo

Warm, ripe, easy reds. Hot, sunny plains and rolling hills, with cork oaks all around.

Alentejo: key grapes Aragonez, Trincadeira.

The grapes

  • Tempranillo (red): cherry, leather, vanilla. Best known in Rioja, Ribera del Duero.
  • Trincadeira (red): plum, blackberry, spice, herbs. Best known in Alentejo, Douro, Tejo.
  • Alicante Bouschet (red): black cherry, plum, chocolate, earth. Best known in Alentejo, Almansa, Languedoc.
  • Cabernet Sauvignon (red): blackcurrant, cedar, graphite. Best known in Bordeaux, Napa Valley, Coonawarra.

More food ideas

  • Steak: Firm tannins in Cabernet and Tempranillo cut through the fat and char of a seared steak, while peppery Shiraz echoes the sauce.
  • Roast leg of lamb: Lamb's rich fat and rosemary call for structured reds; Cabernet's cassis and cedar and Rioja's savoury oak are the classic partners.
  • Beef tacos: Juicy, fruit-forward reds with soft tannins match spiced beef, salsa and cheese, and won't turn bitter against a little chilli.
  • Beef Wellington: Tender fillet and mushroom duxelles suit a structured but polished red; Bordeaux or Burgundy matches the richness without overpowering the pastry.
  • Shakshuka: Spiced tomato and peppers suit a dry rosé or a juicy, low-tannin red that won't clash with the cumin and chilli.
