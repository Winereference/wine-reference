About Macedonia

Northern Greece's largest and most varied wine region, from cool, lake-side Amyndeon to the sea-facing slopes of Epanomi and Chalkidiki. Mostly continental inland, with cold winters, hot summers and big day–night swings; Amyndeon, at around 600–750 m between lakes, is among the coolest vineyard areas in Greece. Near the Aegean (Epanomi, Chalkidiki, Kavala) the climate turns Mediterranean, tempered by sea breezes. Soils range from sand (parts of Amyndeon) to limestone, clay and schist.

Macedonia: key grapes Xinomavro, Malagousia, Assyrtiko, Negoska, Limnio, Syrah, Sauvignon Blanc.

The grape

  • Xinomavro (red): red cherry, tomato leaf, olive. Best known in Naoussa, Greece.

More food ideas

  • Moussaka: Greek reds with bright acidity and spice match the cinnamon-scented lamb and slice through the rich béchamel topping.
  • Lamb kleftiko: Slow-cooked lamb with garlic, lemon and oregano suits Greek reds whose ripe fruit and firm tannins match the meat's richness.
  • Greek saganaki: Salty, pan-fried cheese with lemon suits a crisp, mineral Assyrtiko or a dry Xinomavro rosé that cuts the fat.
