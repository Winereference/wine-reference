Naoussa wine region

Greece · Europe

Northern Greece's classic red appellation on the slopes of Mount Vermio: firm, high-acid, long-lived Xinomavro.

Ancient winemaking and many native grapes, from island whites to northern reds.

CountryGreece
Key grapesXinomavro
SubregionsGiannakochori, Trilofos, Fytia, Rodochori, Stenimachos, Kopanos, Lefkadia, Marina, Anthemia

Climate and soil

Mediterranean with a strong continental streak: cold, sometimes snowy winters, warm summers and cool nights. Vineyards sit on the south-east slopes of Mount Vermio at roughly 150–400 m, with soils that change from plot to plot: clay-loam, sand, marl and limestone.

Key areas and appellations

Giannakochori, Trilofos, Fytia, Rodochori, Stenimachos, Kopanos, Lefkadia, Marina, Anthemia

Good to know

One of Greece's first appellations (1971), for red wine made only from Xinomavro (literally 'acid-black'). Often compared with Nebbiolo: fairly pale, with high acidity, firm tannins and aromas of sour cherry, tomato, olive and dried herbs that turn to leather and spice with age. Top bottles need 8–10 years and can last decades. A new generation of growers now bottles single vineyards and village wines.

The grapes of Naoussa

  • Xinomavro (red): red cherry, tomato leaf, olive. Best known in Naoussa, Greece.

Naoussa wines to try

Kir-Yianni Ramnista

Naoussa · Xinomavro

Greece's answer to Nebbiolo: sour cherry, tomato, olive and firm tannins.

Food: Moussaka, lamb kleftiko, feta dishes

Red · Greece

Thymiopoulos Vineyards Jeunes Vignes de Xinomavro

Naoussa · Xinomavro

Young-vine Xinomavro made for drinking now: red cherry, tomato leaf and spice with bright acidity.

Red · Greece

Boutari Naoussa

Naoussa · Xinomavro

Classic Xinomavro with dried cherry, tomato leaf and spice, firm tannins and a savoury, Nebbiolo-like lift.

Red · Greece

Thymiopoulos Vineyards Earth and Sky

Naoussa · Naoussa PDO · Xinomavro

Old-vine Naoussa Xinomavro: red cherry, rose, olive and spice over grippy tannins and bright acidity.

Food: Moussaka, grilled pork souvlaki

Food to try with Naoussa wines

  • Moussaka: Greek reds with bright acidity and spice match the cinnamon-scented lamb and slice through the rich béchamel topping.
  • Lamb kleftiko: Slow-cooked lamb with garlic, lemon and oregano suits Greek reds whose ripe fruit and firm tannins match the meat's richness.
  • Greek saganaki: Salty, pan-fried cheese with lemon suits a crisp, mineral Assyrtiko or a dry Xinomavro rosé that cuts the fat.
