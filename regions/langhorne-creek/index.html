Langhorne Creek wine region

Australia · Oceania

Flat, river-fed vineyards near Lake Alexandrina in South Australia, known for soft, generous reds and as a backbone of big-brand blends.

CountryAustralia
Key grapesShiraz, Cabernet Sauvignon, Malbec, Verdelho

Climate and soil

Warm, but cooled every afternoon by the "Lake Doctor", a breeze off Lake Alexandrina that slows ripening. Rain is low, so vineyards were long watered by diverting winter floods from the Bremer and Angas rivers across the flats; today lake water is also used. Soils are deep alluvial sandy loams over clay.

Key areas and appellations

Fleurieu zone, Bremer River flats, Angas River flats, Lake Alexandrina shore

Good to know

Frank Potts founded Bleasdale here in 1850, and the family still runs it; its old red-gum lever press is a local landmark. Much of the region's fruit disappears into multi-regional blends from large companies, so the name is less known than the wine. Reds are soft, plummy and approachable early; Malbec and Cabernet do especially well.

The grapes of Langhorne Creek

  • Syrah / Shiraz (red): blackberry, pepper, smoke. Best known in Rhône, Barossa, Washington.
  • Cabernet Sauvignon (red): blackcurrant, cedar, graphite. Best known in Bordeaux, Napa Valley, Coonawarra.
  • Malbec (red): plum, blackberry, violet. Best known in Mendoza, Cahors.
  • Verdelho (white): citrus, tropical, honey. Best known in Madeira, Australia.

Food to try with Langhorne Creek wines

  • Steak: Firm tannins in Cabernet and Tempranillo cut through the fat and char of a seared steak, while peppery Shiraz echoes the sauce.
  • Roast leg of lamb: Lamb's rich fat and rosemary call for structured reds; Cabernet's cassis and cedar and Rioja's savoury oak are the classic partners.
  • Shepherd's pie: Peppery, juicy Grenache and Shiraz match savoury lamb mince and gravy without overwhelming a homely dish.
  • Chilli con carne: Ripe, plush, low-tannin reds like Malbec and Zinfandel soften chilli heat and match the dish's smoky spice and beans.
  • Venison stew: Gamey venison loves Syrah's dark fruit, pepper and smoky notes, and Grenache-led Rhône blends add warmth for a winter stew.
  • Lamb tagine with apricots: Warm spices, apricots and lamb suit ripe, spicy Grenache and Syrah blends, whose soft fruit embraces the sweetness.
