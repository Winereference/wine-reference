Edna Valley wine region

Photo: Madison Detwiler on Unsplash

USA · North America

Fog-cooled valley near San Luis Obispo with one of California's longest growing seasons.

CountryUSA
Key grapesChardonnay, Pinot Noir, Syrah, Grenache

Climate and soil

The valley runs roughly south-east from Morro Bay, so Pacific air and morning fog flow straight in; summers are mild, frosts are rare and the long, slow season lets Chardonnay keep its acidity. Soils are a patchwork of marine sediments, clay loam and weathered volcanic rock from the chain of old volcanic plugs (the Morros) nearby.

Key areas and appellations

San Luis Obispo Coast, Paragon Vineyard, Orcutt Road

Good to know

Became an AVA in 1982, the first in San Luis Obispo County. The Niven family's Paragon Vineyard, planted in 1973, put the valley on the map for Chardonnay; since the 1990s John Alban's estate has made it a reference point for Rhône varieties in America. In 2022 it was also placed within the larger San Luis Obispo Coast AVA.

The grapes of Edna Valley

  • Chardonnay (white): apple, lemon, butter, oak. Best known in Burgundy, California, Australia.
  • Pinot Noir (red): red cherry, earth, mushroom. Best known in Burgundy, Oregon, New Zealand.
  • Syrah / Shiraz (red): blackberry, pepper, smoke. Best known in Rhône, Barossa, Washington.
  • Garnacha / Grenache (red): strawberry, cherry, spice. Best known in Spain, Rhône, Sardinia.

Food to try with Edna Valley wines

  • Steak: Firm tannins in Cabernet and Tempranillo cut through the fat and char of a seared steak, while peppery Shiraz echoes the sauce.
  • Salmon: Salmon is rich enough for a light red: low-tannin Pinot Noir won't clash with the oily fish, and creamy Chardonnay matches its texture.
  • Roast chicken: Roast chicken is a versatile partner; lightly oaked Chardonnay or a soft, low-tannin Pinot Noir or Merlot suits crisp skin and savoury gravy.
  • Beef bourguignon: The beef is braised in red Burgundy, so Pinot Noir mirrors its earthy mushroom and bacon flavours without overpowering the tender meat.
  • Shepherd's pie: Peppery, juicy Grenache and Shiraz match savoury lamb mince and gravy without overwhelming a homely dish.
  • Toad in the hole: Soft, juicy Merlot and Grenache have enough fruit for pork sausages and onion gravy, with gentle tannins that suit the batter.
