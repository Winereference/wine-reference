Rheinhessen wine region

Photo: Babette Landmesser on Unsplash

Germany · Europe

Germany's largest wine region, rolling farmland inside the Rhine's bend, reborn as a source of serious dry Riesling and Silvaner.

Cool-climate wines led by Riesling, graded by ripeness.

CountryGermany
Key grapesRiesling, Müller-Thurgau, Silvaner, Dornfelder, Grauburgunder, Spätburgunder
SubregionsBingen, Nierstein, Wonnegau

Climate and soil

A rolling hill country (the 'land of a thousand hills') protected by the Hunsrück, Taunus and Odenwald, so it is warm and one of Germany's driest regions. Mostly loess, marl and limestone; red slate and sandstone (the Roter Hang) on the steep river slope at Nierstein and Nackenheim; porphyry around Siefersheim.

Key areas and appellations

Bingen, Nierstein, Wonnegau, Roter Hang, Nackenheim, Westhofen, Flörsheim-Dalsheim, Siefersheim, Mainz, Worms, Alzey

Good to know

Once known mainly for Liebfraumilch (named after the Liebfrauenkirche in Worms) and bulk Müller-Thurgau, Rheinhessen now has a generation of quality growers making some of Germany's most sought-after dry Riesling. It is also the world's largest Silvaner region, and the grape Scheurebe was bred here, at Alzey.

The grapes of Rheinhessen

  • Riesling (white): peach, lime, petrol, honey. Best known in Mosel, Alsace, Finger Lakes.
  • Müller-Thurgau (white): pear, floral, peach. Best known in Germany.
  • Silvaner (white): apple, herbs, mineral. Best known in Franken.
  • Dornfelder (red): black cherry, plum, spice. Best known in Germany.

Rheinhessen · Riesling

Organic, biodynamic dry Riesling with ripe citrus, apricot and a juicy, limestone-fresh line.

Food: Chicken with tarragon, asparagus with hollandaise

White · Germany

Weingut Keller Von der Fels Riesling

Rheinhessen · Riesling

Limestone-grown dry Riesling: lemon zest, apricot and crushed stone, polished and precise with a quietly powerful finish.

White · Germany

Weingut Gunderloch Jean-Baptiste Riesling Kabinett

Rheinhessen · Riesling

Off-dry Kabinett from red slate of the Roter Hang: apricot, lime and a whisper of sweetness balanced by racy acidity.

Food: Pork belly bao, Szechuan prawns

Food to try with Rheinhessen wines

  • Spicy curry: A touch of sweetness and low alcohol in off-dry Riesling cool chilli heat, while fruity rosé handles aromatic spice without turning bitter.
  • Cheese board: Mixed cheeses need a mixed line-up: Riesling for soft and blue cheeses, soft Merlot for hard cheeses, and Prosecco's bubbles to refresh.
  • Pad Thai: Sweet, sour, salty and spicy flavours suit aromatic whites; Riesling's lime acidity and Gewürztraminer's lychee match tamarind and peanuts.
  • Thai green curry: Coconut, lemongrass and green chilli suit off-dry, aromatic whites whose touch of sweetness tames heat and echoes the herbs.
  • Kung pao chicken: Sichuan pepper, chilli and peanuts need fruity, refreshing wines; off-dry Riesling and fuller rosé handle the heat and sweet-sour glaze.
  • Slow-roast pork belly: Bright acidity in Riesling or Chenin slices through fatty pork and crackling, while a touch of fruit sweetness flatters the meat.
