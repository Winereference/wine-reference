Mendocino wine region

Photo: David Klein on Unsplash

USA · North America

California's northernmost major wine county: foggy Anderson Valley for Pinot and sparkling, warm inland valleys for old-vine Zinfandel and Carignan.

CountryUSA
Key grapesZinfandel, Pinot Noir, Chardonnay, Petite Sirah, Carignan
SubregionsAnderson Valley, Mendocino Ridge, Redwood Valley, Potter Valley, McDowell Valley, Cole Ranch, Yorkville Highlands

Climate and soil

Two very different climates. West of the coastal hills, the Navarro River funnels Pacific fog into Anderson Valley, giving a cool, long season for Pinot Noir, Chardonnay, Gewürztraminer and sparkling base wine. Inland around Ukiah, Redwood Valley and Hopland summers are hot and dry, ideal for Zinfandel, Petite Sirah and dry-farmed old Carignan. Mendocino Ridge vineyards sit above the fog line.

Key areas and appellations

Mendocino, Anderson Valley, Mendocino Ridge, Redwood Valley, Potter Valley, McDowell Valley, Cole Ranch, Yorkville Highlands, Ukiah Valley

Good to know

The Mendocino AVA (1984) covers the southern part of the county; Anderson Valley (1983) is older. Mendocino Ridge (1997) is unusual: its vineyards must lie above 1,200 ft, so the AVA is a scatter of ridge-top islands. The county was an early leader in organic farming, and Roederer and Scharffenberger proved Anderson Valley for traditional-method sparkling wine.

The grapes of Mendocino

  • Zinfandel (red): blackberry, plum, pepper. Best known in California.
  • Pinot Noir (red): red cherry, earth, mushroom. Best known in Burgundy, Oregon, New Zealand.
  • Chardonnay (white): apple, lemon, butter, oak. Best known in Burgundy, California, Australia.
  • Petite Sirah (red): blackberry, plum, pepper. Best known in California.
  • Carignan (red): blackberry, cherry, herbs. Best known in Languedoc, Sardinia.

Food to try with Mendocino wines

  • Salmon: Salmon is rich enough for a light red: low-tannin Pinot Noir won't clash with the oily fish, and creamy Chardonnay matches its texture.
  • Roast chicken: Roast chicken is a versatile partner; lightly oaked Chardonnay or a soft, low-tannin Pinot Noir or Merlot suits crisp skin and savoury gravy.
  • Beef bourguignon: The beef is braised in red Burgundy, so Pinot Noir mirrors its earthy mushroom and bacon flavours without overpowering the tender meat.
  • Chilli con carne: Ripe, plush, low-tannin reds like Malbec and Zinfandel soften chilli heat and match the dish's smoky spice and beans.
  • Coq au vin: Red Burgundy or Beaujolais suits chicken braised in wine with mushrooms and lardons, adding red-berry fruit and earthy notes.
  • Duck breast with cherry sauce: Pinot Noir's cherry fruit echoes the sauce and its acidity cuts rich duck fat, while plush Merlot suits the sweeter side of the dish.
