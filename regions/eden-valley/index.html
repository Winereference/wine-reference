Eden Valley wine region

Australia · Oceania

The cool, rocky high country above the Barossa: taut Riesling and elegant old-vine Shiraz.

CountryAustralia
Key grapesRiesling, Shiraz, Chardonnay, Viognier
SubregionsHigh Eden

Climate and soil

Higher (roughly 400–550 m), cooler and windier than the Barossa Valley floor next door, with lower rainfall in places and a later harvest. The hills are rocky: weathered granite, schist and ironstone with thin sandy and gravelly loams, which keep yields low.

Key areas and appellations

Barossa zone, High Eden, Springton, Keyneton, Pewsey Vale

Good to know

Eden Valley and the Barossa Valley together make up the Barossa zone. Pewsey Vale was first planted in 1847, and Henschke's Hill of Grace Shiraz comes from vines near Keyneton planted in the 19th century. Yalumba helped put Australian Viognier on the map here. Riesling is lean and lime-driven; Shiraz is fresher and more peppery than on the valley floor.

The grapes of Eden Valley

  • Riesling (white): peach, lime, petrol, honey. Best known in Mosel, Alsace, Finger Lakes.
  • Syrah / Shiraz (red): blackberry, pepper, smoke. Best known in Rhône, Barossa, Washington.
  • Chardonnay (white): apple, lemon, butter, oak. Best known in Burgundy, California, Australia.
  • Viognier (white): peach, apricot, honeysuckle. Best known in Rhône.

Food to try with Eden Valley wines

  • Steak: Firm tannins in Cabernet and Tempranillo cut through the fat and char of a seared steak, while peppery Shiraz echoes the sauce.
  • Salmon: Salmon is rich enough for a light red: low-tannin Pinot Noir won't clash with the oily fish, and creamy Chardonnay matches its texture.
  • Spicy curry: A touch of sweetness and low alcohol in off-dry Riesling cool chilli heat, while fruity rosé handles aromatic spice without turning bitter.
  • Roast chicken: Roast chicken is a versatile partner; lightly oaked Chardonnay or a soft, low-tannin Pinot Noir or Merlot suits crisp skin and savoury gravy.
  • Cheese board: Mixed cheeses need a mixed line-up: Riesling for soft and blue cheeses, soft Merlot for hard cheeses, and Prosecco's bubbles to refresh.
  • Shepherd's pie: Peppery, juicy Grenache and Shiraz match savoury lamb mince and gravy without overwhelming a homely dish.
