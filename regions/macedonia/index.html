Macedonia wine region

Greece · Europe

Northern Greece's largest and most varied wine region, from cool, lake-side Amyndeon to the sea-facing slopes of Epanomi and Chalkidiki.

Ancient winemaking and many native grapes, from island whites to northern reds.

CountryGreece
Key grapesXinomavro, Malagousia, Assyrtiko, Negoska, Limnio, Syrah, Sauvignon Blanc
SubregionsNaoussa, Amyndeon, Goumenissa, Slopes of Meliton

Climate and soil

Mostly continental inland, with cold winters, hot summers and big day–night swings; Amyndeon, at around 600–750 m between lakes, is among the coolest vineyard areas in Greece. Near the Aegean (Epanomi, Chalkidiki, Kavala) the climate turns Mediterranean, tempered by sea breezes. Soils range from sand (parts of Amyndeon) to limestone, clay and schist.

Key areas and appellations

Naoussa, Amyndeon, Goumenissa, Slopes of Meliton, Epanomi, Drama, Kavala, Pangeon, Florina, Mount Athos

Good to know

Xinomavro is the signature red, but PDO rules differ: Naoussa is pure Xinomavro, Goumenissa blends it with the softer, fruitier Negoska, and Amyndeon also allows still and sparkling rosé. Ktima Gerovassiliou at Epanomi rescued the white Malagousia from near extinction, and Drama and Kavala are known for polished blends of Greek and international grapes.

The grapes of Macedonia

  • Xinomavro (red): red cherry, tomato leaf, olive. Best known in Naoussa, Greece.
  • Malagousia (white): peach, citrus, herbs. Best known in Greece.
  • Assyrtiko (white): lemon, mineral, saline. Best known in Santorini.
  • Limnio (red): red cherry, bay leaf, herbs, spice. Best known in Lemnos, Chalkidiki, Mount Athos.
  • Syrah / Shiraz (red): blackberry, pepper, smoke. Best known in Rhône, Barossa, Washington.
  • Sauvignon Blanc (white): grapefruit, lime, fresh herbs. Best known in Loire, Marlborough, Bordeaux.

Macedonia wines to try

White · Greece

Ktima Gerovassiliou Malagousia

Macedonia · PGI Epanomi · Malagousia

The estate that revived Malagousia: jasmine, ripe peach and basil with a round, fresh, lightly saline palate.

Food: Grilled octopus, spanakopita

Red · Greece

Alpha Estate Xinomavro Reserve Vieilles Vignes

Macedonia · Amyndeon PDO · Xinomavro

Ungrafted old vines on a cool plateau: sour cherry, sun-dried tomato and spice, with firm tannins and a long, savoury finish.

Food: Slow-roast lamb kleftiko, mushroom stifado

White · Greece

Ktima Biblia Chora Ovilos White

Macedonia · PGI Pangeon · Assyrtiko, Sémillon

Barrel-aged Assyrtiko with Sémillon: lemon, beeswax and toasted nuts layered over a tense, saline core.

Food: Grilled sea bass, roast chicken with lemon

Food to try with Macedonia wines

  • Steak: Firm tannins in Cabernet and Tempranillo cut through the fat and char of a seared steak, while peppery Shiraz echoes the sauce.
  • Oysters: Briny oysters love crisp, mineral whites: Champagne's bubbles, Muscadet's saline edge and Sancerre's citrus all act like a squeeze of lemon.
  • Shepherd's pie: Peppery, juicy Grenache and Shiraz match savoury lamb mince and gravy without overwhelming a homely dish.
  • Venison stew: Gamey venison loves Syrah's dark fruit, pepper and smoky notes, and Grenache-led Rhône blends add warmth for a winter stew.
  • Chicken Kiev: Garlic butter and crisp crumbs want a fresh white; Chablis-style Chardonnay or zesty Sauvignon Blanc cut the richness and match the herbs.
  • Fish and chips: Crisp acidity and bubbles slice through batter like a squeeze of lemon, which is why fizz with fish and chips is a British favourite.
