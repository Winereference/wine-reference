Beaujolais wine region

Photo: Carnet de Voyage d'Alex on Unsplash

France · Europe

Gamay country: juicy Beaujolais Nouveau at one end, ten granite-grown crus at the other.

The benchmark for many wine styles, organised by appellation (AOC).

CountryFrance
Key grapesGamay, Chardonnay
SubregionsBrouilly, Côte de Brouilly, Régnié, Morgon, Chiroubles, Fleurie, Moulin-à-Vent, Chénas, Juliénas, Saint-Amour

Climate and soil

Semi-continental, with warm summers and Mediterranean touches from the Rhône corridor to the south. The northern hills, home of the crus, are pink granite and schist, with blue volcanic diorite on Mont Brouilly; the flatter south (the Pierres Dorées) has clay and golden limestone.

Key areas and appellations

Brouilly, Côte de Brouilly, Régnié, Morgon, Chiroubles, Fleurie, Moulin-à-Vent, Chénas, Juliénas, Saint-Amour, Beaujolais-Villages, Beaujolais

Good to know

Gamay is often made by whole-bunch, semi-carbonic fermentation, which gives the crunchy, fragrant fruit the region is known for. Beaujolais Nouveau is released on the third Thursday of November. Morgon's Côte du Py and Moulin-à-Vent produce crus that age for a decade or more, and the 'Gang of Four' (Lapierre, Foillard, Thévenet, Breton) helped launch the natural-wine movement here in the 1980s.

The grapes of Beaujolais

  • Gamay (red): red cherry, strawberry, floral. Best known in Beaujolais.
  • Chardonnay (white): apple, lemon, butter, oak. Best known in Burgundy, California, Australia.

Food to try with Beaujolais wines

  • Salmon: Salmon is rich enough for a light red: low-tannin Pinot Noir won't clash with the oily fish, and creamy Chardonnay matches its texture.
  • Roast chicken: Roast chicken is a versatile partner; lightly oaked Chardonnay or a soft, low-tannin Pinot Noir or Merlot suits crisp skin and savoury gravy.
  • Beef bourguignon: The beef is braised in red Burgundy, so Pinot Noir mirrors its earthy mushroom and bacon flavours without overpowering the tender meat.
  • Coq au vin: Red Burgundy or Beaujolais suits chicken braised in wine with mushrooms and lardons, adding red-berry fruit and earthy notes.
  • Roast turkey: Lean turkey and its many trimmings need light, fruity, fresh wines; Pinot Noir, Beaujolais or Chablis won't overpower the meat.
  • Chicken Kiev: Garlic butter and crisp crumbs want a fresh white; Chablis-style Chardonnay or zesty Sauvignon Blanc cut the richness and match the herbs.
