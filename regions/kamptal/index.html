Kamptal wine region

Austria · Europe

Lower Austria's valley of the Kamp around Langenlois: peppery Grüner Veltliner on loess and taut, stony Riesling from the Heiligenstein.

Crisp whites led by Grüner Veltliner, organized by the DAC system.

CountryAustria
Key grapesGrüner Veltliner, Riesling, Zweigelt, Pinot Noir, Chardonnay

Climate and soil

Where the Kamp river leaves the cooler Waldviertel plateau and opens onto the Danube plain: hot Pannonian days alternate with cold air sinking from the north at night, keeping aromas fresh. Deep loess and gravel on the lower slopes and terraces; ancient primary rock (gneiss, mica schist) and the Heiligenstein's Permian desert sandstone and volcanic debris higher up.

Key areas and appellations

Langenlois, Zöbing, Kammern, Gobelsburg, Straß im Straßertale, Schönberg, Heiligenstein, Lamm, Gaisberg, Käferberg, Loiserberg, Steinmaßl

Good to know

Kamptal DAC (from the 2008 vintage) covers Grüner Veltliner and Riesling in three tiers: regional wine, village wine (Ortswein) and single-vineyard wine (Riedenwein, marked "Ried"). Other grapes are sold as Niederösterreich. Langenlois is Austria's largest wine town.

The grapes of Kamptal

  • Grüner Veltliner (white): green apple, pepper, lime. Best known in Austria.
  • Riesling (white): peach, lime, petrol, honey. Best known in Mosel, Alsace, Finger Lakes.
  • Zweigelt (red): cherry, plum, pepper. Best known in Austria.
  • Pinot Noir (red): red cherry, earth, mushroom. Best known in Burgundy, Oregon, New Zealand.
  • Chardonnay (white): apple, lemon, butter, oak. Best known in Burgundy, California, Australia.

Food to try with Kamptal wines

  • Salmon: Salmon is rich enough for a light red: low-tannin Pinot Noir won't clash with the oily fish, and creamy Chardonnay matches its texture.
  • Spicy curry: A touch of sweetness and low alcohol in off-dry Riesling cool chilli heat, while fruity rosé handles aromatic spice without turning bitter.
  • Roast chicken: Roast chicken is a versatile partner; lightly oaked Chardonnay or a soft, low-tannin Pinot Noir or Merlot suits crisp skin and savoury gravy.
  • Cheese board: Mixed cheeses need a mixed line-up: Riesling for soft and blue cheeses, soft Merlot for hard cheeses, and Prosecco's bubbles to refresh.
  • Beef bourguignon: The beef is braised in red Burgundy, so Pinot Noir mirrors its earthy mushroom and bacon flavours without overpowering the tender meat.
  • Coq au vin: Red Burgundy or Beaujolais suits chicken braised in wine with mushrooms and lardons, adding red-berry fruit and earthy notes.
