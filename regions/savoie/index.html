Savoie wine region

France · Europe

Scattered Alpine vineyards between Lake Geneva and the Isère valley: light, crisp mountain whites, peppery Mondeuse reds and a growing range of crémant.

The benchmark for many wine styles, organised by appellation (AOC).

CountryFrance
Key grapesJacquère, Altesse, Bergeron (Roussanne), Chasselas, Gringet, Mondeuse, Gamay
SubregionsVin de Savoie, Roussette de Savoie, Seyssel, Crémant de Savoie

Climate and soil

Mountain climate softened by the large lakes (Geneva, Le Bourget) and by sheltered, south- and south-east-facing slopes, mostly at 250–500 m. Soils are glacial moraine, limestone scree and marl; Apremont and Abymes sit on debris from the great 1248 landslide of Mont Granier.

Key areas and appellations

Vin de Savoie (with named crus such as Apremont, Abymes, Chignin, Chignin-Bergeron, Arbin, Jongieux, Cruet, Ayze, Crépy, Marignan, Ripaille, Marin), Roussette de Savoie, Seyssel, Crémant de Savoie

Good to know

Jacquère is the everyday white: low in alcohol, lemony and alpine-fresh, the classic match for fondue and raclette. Altesse (sold as Roussette de Savoie) makes richer, longer-lived whites, Chignin-Bergeron is local Roussanne, and the near-extinct Gringet survives around Ayze. Mondeuse gives deep-coloured, peppery reds. Vineyards are small and fragmented, so little of the wine leaves the region.

The grapes of Savoie

  • Jacquère (white): lemon, green apple, white flowers, flint. Best known in Savoie.
  • Altesse (white): bergamot, quince, almond, honey. Best known in Savoie, Bugey.
  • Chasselas (white): apple, citrus, mineral. Best known in Switzerland.
  • Mondeuse (red): black cherry, plum, pepper, violet. Best known in Savoie, Bugey.
  • Gamay (red): red cherry, strawberry, floral. Best known in Beaujolais.

Savoie wines to try

White · France

Domaine Dupasquier Roussette de Savoie Marestel

Savoie · Roussette de Savoie Marestel · Altesse

Alpine Altesse from steep Jongieux slopes: quince, bergamot and hazelnut, released with some age and built to keep developing.

Food: Tartiflette, Beaufort cheese

White · France

Domaine des Ardoisières Quartz

Savoie · IGP Vin des Allobroges · Altesse

Crystalline Altesse from schist terraces above the Isère: pear, mountain herbs and a long, chalky, mineral finish.

Food: Lake fish with beurre blanc, Comté

White · France

André & Michel Quenard Chignin-Bergeron Les Terrasses

Savoie · Vin de Savoie Chignin-Bergeron · Roussanne

Generous mountain Roussanne: apricot, honeysuckle and toasted almond, rounded but kept fresh by Alpine acidity.

Food: Chicken in morel cream, fondue

Food to try with Savoie wines

  • Beef bourguignon: The beef is braised in red Burgundy, so Pinot Noir mirrors its earthy mushroom and bacon flavours without overpowering the tender meat.
  • Coq au vin: Red Burgundy or Beaujolais suits chicken braised in wine with mushrooms and lardons, adding red-berry fruit and earthy notes.
  • Roast turkey: Lean turkey and its many trimmings need light, fruity, fresh wines; Pinot Noir, Beaujolais or Chablis won't overpower the meat.
  • Cheese fondue: In Switzerland fondue comes with the local white: light, dry Chasselas cleanses the palate between mouthfuls of melted Gruyère.
  • Baked camembert: Gooey camembert needs acidity: crisp unoaked Chablis cuts the richness, while light, fruity Beaujolais suits garlic and rosemary.
  • Welsh rarebit: Mustardy, ale-laced cheddar on toast needs bright acidity; juicy Beaujolais or crisp English sparkling cuts the richness.
