Burgenland wine region

Austria · Europe

Austria's warm eastern edge on the Hungarian border: serious Blaufränkisch and world-class botrytis sweet wines around Lake Neusiedl.

Crisp whites led by Grüner Veltliner, organized by the DAC system.

CountryAustria
Key grapesBlaufränkisch, Zweigelt, St. Laurent, Welschriesling, Pinot Blanc, Chardonnay, Furmint
SubregionsNeusiedlersee, Leithaberg, Rosalia, Mittelburgenland, Eisenberg, Ruster Ausbruch

Climate and soil

Pannonian climate: hot, dry summers, cold winters and over 300 days of sun a year. The vast, shallow Lake Neusiedl moderates temperatures and, with autumn mists, encourages noble rot. Soils vary widely: limestone and schist on the Leitha hills, heavy loam in Mittelburgenland, iron-rich schist on the Eisenberg, sand and gravel in the Seewinkel.

Key areas and appellations

Neusiedlersee DAC, Leithaberg DAC, Rosalia DAC, Mittelburgenland DAC, Eisenberg DAC, Ruster Ausbruch DAC, Seewinkel, Rust

Good to know

Austria's red-wine heartland: Blaufränkisch is the signature grape, from spicy, structured Mittelburgenland to mineral Leithaberg and Eisenberg. Around Illmitz and the Seewinkel the lake's humidity makes Beerenauslese and Trockenbeerenauslese an annual possibility, and Rust's Ausbruch is one of the world's historic sweet wines, protected as its own DAC since 2020. Mittelburgenland (2005) was Burgenland's first DAC.

The grapes of Burgenland

  • Blaufränkisch (red): blackberry, cherry, pepper. Best known in Burgenland.
  • Zweigelt (red): cherry, plum, pepper. Best known in Austria.
  • St. Laurent (red): cherry, plum, spice. Best known in Austria.
  • Welschriesling (white): apple, citrus, herbs. Best known in Austria.
  • Pinot Blanc (white): apple, pear, almond. Best known in Alsace, Alto Adige.
  • Chardonnay (white): apple, lemon, butter, oak. Best known in Burgundy, California, Australia.
  • Furmint (white): apple, pear, honey. Best known in Tokaj.

Burgenland wines to try

Red · Austria

Weingut Moric Blaufränkisch

Burgenland · Blaufränkisch

Fine-boned Blaufränkisch with sour cherry, black pepper and graphite, more about tension and perfume than weight.

Food: Venison medallions, duck with red cabbage

Sweet · Austria

Kracher Cuvée Beerenauslese

Burgenland · Welschriesling, Chardonnay

Botrytised sweet wine from the shores of Neusiedlersee, with candied apricot, honey and pineapple lifted by fresh acidity.

Food: Apricot tart, blue cheese

Red · Austria

Weingut Prieler Leithaberg Blaufränkisch

Burgenland · Leithaberg DAC · Blaufränkisch

Dark cherry, blueberry and graphite on a cool, mineral frame, with fine-grained tannins and a spicy, lingering tail.

Food: Venison ragout, roast goose

Food to try with Burgenland wines

  • Salmon: Salmon is rich enough for a light red: low-tannin Pinot Noir won't clash with the oily fish, and creamy Chardonnay matches its texture.
  • Roast chicken: Roast chicken is a versatile partner; lightly oaked Chardonnay or a soft, low-tannin Pinot Noir or Merlot suits crisp skin and savoury gravy.
  • Roast turkey: Lean turkey and its many trimmings need light, fruity, fresh wines; Pinot Noir, Beaujolais or Chablis won't overpower the meat.
  • Chicken Kiev: Garlic butter and crisp crumbs want a fresh white; Chablis-style Chardonnay or zesty Sauvignon Blanc cut the richness and match the herbs.
  • Fish pie: Creamy sauce and buttery mash need a rounded white with fresh acidity, such as unoaked Burgundy Chardonnay or Chenin Blanc.
  • Butternut squash soup: Velvety squash matches full-bodied whites; Viognier's apricot and Chardonnay's creamy texture echo the soup's sweetness.
