Nagambie Lakes wine region

Australia · Oceania

Goulburn Valley subregion in central Victoria shaped by its lakes and lagoons, home of Tahbilk's historic Marsanne and Shiraz.

CountryAustralia
Key grapesShiraz, Marsanne, Cabernet Sauvignon, Viognier, Roussanne

Climate and soil

Warm and fairly dry inland climate, moderated by Lake Nagambie (formed by the Goulburn Weir), the Goulburn River and its lagoons and billabongs, which cool summer days and reduce frost. Soils are mostly red and brown sandy loams, with patches rich in ironstone gravel.

Key areas and appellations

Goulburn Valley region, Central Victoria zone, Tabilk, Nagambie

Good to know

A subregion of the Goulburn Valley, distinguished largely by the influence of its water. Tahbilk (founded 1860) still farms ungrafted Shiraz planted in 1860 and holds one of the world's largest plantings of Marsanne, sold young and as aged museum releases. Mitchelton, established nearby in the late 1960s, is the other landmark estate. Rhône varieties suit the region well.

The grapes of Nagambie Lakes

  • Syrah / Shiraz (red): blackberry, pepper, smoke. Best known in Rhône, Barossa, Washington.
  • Marsanne (white): pear, almond, honey. Best known in Rhône.
  • Cabernet Sauvignon (red): blackcurrant, cedar, graphite. Best known in Bordeaux, Napa Valley, Coonawarra.
  • Viognier (white): peach, apricot, honeysuckle. Best known in Rhône.
  • Roussanne (white): pear, herbs, honey. Best known in Rhône.

Notable producers in Nagambie Lakes

  • Tahbilk
  • Mitchelton
  • Dalfarras

Food to try with Nagambie Lakes wines

  • Steak: Firm tannins in Cabernet and Tempranillo cut through the fat and char of a seared steak, while peppery Shiraz echoes the sauce.
  • Roast leg of lamb: Lamb's rich fat and rosemary call for structured reds; Cabernet's cassis and cedar and Rioja's savoury oak are the classic partners.
  • Shepherd's pie: Peppery, juicy Grenache and Shiraz match savoury lamb mince and gravy without overwhelming a homely dish.
  • Venison stew: Gamey venison loves Syrah's dark fruit, pepper and smoky notes, and Grenache-led Rhône blends add warmth for a winter stew.
  • Butternut squash soup: Velvety squash matches full-bodied whites; Viognier's apricot and Chardonnay's creamy texture echo the soup's sweetness.
  • Lamb tagine with apricots: Warm spices, apricots and lamb suit ripe, spicy Grenache and Syrah blends, whose soft fruit embraces the sweetness.
