Coonawarra wine region

Australia · Oceania

Australia's Cabernet capital: a thin strip of red terra rossa soil in South Australia's far south-east.

CountryAustralia
Key grapesCabernet Sauvignon, Shiraz, Merlot

Climate and soil

Cool for South Australia, with the Southern Ocean less than 100 km away bringing mild summers, chilly nights and a long, slow ripening season; spring frost is a real hazard on the flat land. The famous terra rossa is a shallow layer of iron-rich red clay loam over soft limestone, with a high water table below; patches of black, poorly drained soil lie alongside it.

Key areas and appellations

Limestone Coast zone, Penola, terra rossa strip

Good to know

Settled in 1890 as John Riddoch's Penola Fruit Colony, and the triple-gabled winery he built is now Wynns Coonawarra Estate. Where exactly the GI boundary should run was fought over in the courts for years, because the red soil sells the wine. Expect blackcurrant, mint and cedar, with firm, fine tannins that age well.

The grapes of Coonawarra

  • Cabernet Sauvignon (red): blackcurrant, cedar, graphite. Best known in Bordeaux, Napa Valley, Coonawarra.
  • Syrah / Shiraz (red): blackberry, pepper, smoke. Best known in Rhône, Barossa, Washington.
  • Merlot (red): plum, cherry, chocolate. Best known in Bordeaux, Tuscany, Washington.

Food to try with Coonawarra wines

  • Steak: Firm tannins in Cabernet and Tempranillo cut through the fat and char of a seared steak, while peppery Shiraz echoes the sauce.
  • Roast chicken: Roast chicken is a versatile partner; lightly oaked Chardonnay or a soft, low-tannin Pinot Noir or Merlot suits crisp skin and savoury gravy.
  • Cheese board: Mixed cheeses need a mixed line-up: Riesling for soft and blue cheeses, soft Merlot for hard cheeses, and Prosecco's bubbles to refresh.
  • Roast leg of lamb: Lamb's rich fat and rosemary call for structured reds; Cabernet's cassis and cedar and Rioja's savoury oak are the classic partners.
  • Shepherd's pie: Peppery, juicy Grenache and Shiraz match savoury lamb mince and gravy without overwhelming a homely dish.
  • Toad in the hole: Soft, juicy Merlot and Grenache have enough fruit for pork sausages and onion gravy, with gentle tannins that suit the batter.
