Tasmania wine region

Australia · Oceania

Australia's coolest wine state, an island south of Victoria whose slow-ripening Pinot Noir, Chardonnay and long-aged sparkling wines are among the country's most sought after.

CountryAustralia
Key grapesPinot Noir, Chardonnay, Sauvignon Blanc, Pinot Gris, Riesling

Climate and soil

Cool maritime climate with a long, mild growing season and plenty of sunshine, so grapes ripen slowly and keep high natural acidity. Most vineyards lie in the north (Tamar Valley, Pipers River) and the drier south-east (Coal River and Derwent valleys) and on the sheltered East Coast. Soils include basalt-derived red earths, dolerite, sandstone and river gravels.

Key areas and appellations

Tamar Valley, Pipers River, Coal River Valley, Derwent Valley, Huon Valley and D'Entrecasteaux Channel, East Coast, North West

Good to know

The whole island is a single GI with no official subregions; the names above are used informally. Pinot Noir is by far the most planted grape, and a large share of the crop goes into traditional-method sparkling wine, much of it bought by mainland houses. The island makes only a small slice of Australia's wine, but fetches some of the country's highest grape prices.

The grapes of Tasmania

  • Pinot Noir (red): red cherry, earth, mushroom. Best known in Burgundy, Oregon, New Zealand.
  • Chardonnay (white): apple, lemon, butter, oak. Best known in Burgundy, California, Australia.
  • Sauvignon Blanc (white): grapefruit, lime, fresh herbs. Best known in Loire, Marlborough, Bordeaux.
  • Riesling (white): peach, lime, petrol, honey. Best known in Mosel, Alsace, Finger Lakes.

Tasmania wines to try

Red · Australia

Tolpuddle Vineyard Pinot Noir

Tasmania · Tasmania (Coal River Valley) · Pinot Noir

Cool-climate Pinot of real finesse: red cherry, rose petal and forest floor over silky tannins and bright acidity.

Food: Duck breast, mushroom risotto

Sparkling · Australia

House of Arras Grand Vintage

Tasmania · Chardonnay, Pinot Noir

Long lees ageing gives brioche, roasted nuts and citrus peel to a fine, creamy mousse with a razor-sharp finish.

Food: Oysters, crab tart

Sparkling · Australia

Jansz Tasmania Premium Cuvée

Tasmania · Chardonnay, Pinot Noir

Bright, good-value traditional-method fizz with green apple, lemon curd and a soft hint of toast.

Food: Smoked salmon blinis, tempura prawns

Food to try with Tasmania wines

  • Salmon: Salmon is rich enough for a light red: low-tannin Pinot Noir won't clash with the oily fish, and creamy Chardonnay matches its texture.
  • Spicy curry: A touch of sweetness and low alcohol in off-dry Riesling cool chilli heat, while fruity rosé handles aromatic spice without turning bitter.
  • Oysters: Briny oysters love crisp, mineral whites: Champagne's bubbles, Muscadet's saline edge and Sancerre's citrus all act like a squeeze of lemon.
  • Roast chicken: Roast chicken is a versatile partner; lightly oaked Chardonnay or a soft, low-tannin Pinot Noir or Merlot suits crisp skin and savoury gravy.
  • Cheese board: Mixed cheeses need a mixed line-up: Riesling for soft and blue cheeses, soft Merlot for hard cheeses, and Prosecco's bubbles to refresh.
  • Beef bourguignon: The beef is braised in red Burgundy, so Pinot Noir mirrors its earthy mushroom and bacon flavours without overpowering the tender meat.
