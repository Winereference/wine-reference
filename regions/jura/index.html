Jura wine region

France · Europe

Small, singular region in the foothills between Burgundy and Switzerland: nutty vin jaune aged under flor, pale, perfumed reds and taut whites on marl.

The benchmark for many wine styles, organised by appellation (AOC).

CountryFrance
Key grapesSavagnin, Chardonnay, Poulsard, Trousseau, Pinot Noir
SubregionsArbois, Château-Chalon, L'Étoile, Côtes du Jura

Climate and soil

Continental climate with cold winters, warm summers and plenty of rain; vineyards face west on the slopes between the Bresse plain and the Jura mountains, mostly at 250–450 m. Soils are largely Triassic and Jurassic marls (grey, blue and red) with limestone outcrops and scree.

Key areas and appellations

Arbois, Arbois-Pupillin, Château-Chalon, L'Étoile, Côtes du Jura, Crémant du Jura, Macvin du Jura

Good to know

Whites come in two families: topped-up (ouillé) wines made like Burgundy, and oxidative 'typé' wines aged under a film of yeast (sous voile). Vin jaune, from Savagnin, must spend at least six years and three months in barrel under that veil and is sold in the squat 62 cl clavelin; Château-Chalon makes only vin jaune. Vin de paille is a sweet wine from grapes dried for months, and Macvin is grape must fortified with local marc.

The grapes of Jura

  • Savagnin (white): apple, walnut, spice. Best known in Jura.
  • Chardonnay (white): apple, lemon, butter, oak. Best known in Burgundy, California, Australia.
  • Poulsard (red): red currant, cranberry, rose petal, earth. Best known in Jura, Arbois.
  • Trousseau (red): red berry, earth, spice. Best known in Jura, California.
  • Pinot Noir (red): red cherry, earth, mushroom. Best known in Burgundy, Oregon, New Zealand.

Jura wines to try

White · France

Domaine Tissot Arbois Chardonnay Patchwork

Jura · Arbois AOC · Chardonnay

A blend of small parcels on varied soils: lemon curd, hazelnut and a flinty, mineral line held taut by bright acidity.

Food: Comté, chicken with morels

White · France

Domaine Berthet-Bondet Château-Chalon

Jura · Château-Chalon AOC · Savagnin

Vin jaune aged under a veil of yeast for over six years: walnut, curry spice and dried citrus with a bone-dry, endless finish.

Food: Aged Comté, poulet de Bresse au vin jaune

White · France

Domaine de Montbourgeau L'Étoile

Jura · L'Étoile AOC · Chardonnay

Chardonnay from limestone and marl around the star-shaped fossils of L'Étoile: green apple, almond and a gently saline edge.

Food: Trout with brown butter, mild Morbier

Food to try with Jura wines

  • Salmon: Salmon is rich enough for a light red: low-tannin Pinot Noir won't clash with the oily fish, and creamy Chardonnay matches its texture.
  • Roast chicken: Roast chicken is a versatile partner; lightly oaked Chardonnay or a soft, low-tannin Pinot Noir or Merlot suits crisp skin and savoury gravy.
  • Beef bourguignon: The beef is braised in red Burgundy, so Pinot Noir mirrors its earthy mushroom and bacon flavours without overpowering the tender meat.
  • Coq au vin: Red Burgundy or Beaujolais suits chicken braised in wine with mushrooms and lardons, adding red-berry fruit and earthy notes.
  • Duck breast with cherry sauce: Pinot Noir's cherry fruit echoes the sauce and its acidity cuts rich duck fat, while plush Merlot suits the sweeter side of the dish.
  • Roast turkey: Lean turkey and its many trimmings need light, fruity, fresh wines; Pinot Noir, Beaujolais or Chablis won't overpower the meat.
