Nemea wine region

Greece · Europe

Greece's largest red-wine appellation, all Agiorgitiko, grown from valley floor to high plateau in the north-east Peloponnese.

Ancient winemaking and many native grapes, from island whites to northern reds.

CountryGreece
Key grapesAgiorgitiko
SubregionsKoutsi, Asprokambos, Gymno, Ancient Nemea, Leontio, Psari, Petri, Ancient Kleones

Climate and soil

Mediterranean at lower levels, turning more continental on the higher slopes, with cold winters and big day-night swings. Vineyards climb from around 250 m to more than 800 m; soils range from deep alluvial and red clay on the valley floor to stony limestone and marl higher up.

Key areas and appellations

Koutsi, Asprokambos, Gymno, Ancient Nemea, Leontio, Psari, Petri, Ancient Kleones

Good to know

One of Greece's original appellations (1971). Only Agiorgitiko is allowed, and it is often called the 'Blood of Hercules' after the myth of the Nemean lion. Growers talk of three altitude bands: the warm valley floor gives soft, fruity wines, the middle slopes the most balanced, and the cool high plateau (Asprokambos) fresher wines and rosé. Koutsi is widely seen as the finest single area.

The grapes of Nemea

  • Agiorgitiko (red): red cherry, plum, spice. Best known in Nemea, Greece.

Food to try with Nemea wines

  • Moussaka: Greek reds with bright acidity and spice match the cinnamon-scented lamb and slice through the rich béchamel topping.
  • Lamb kleftiko: Slow-cooked lamb with garlic, lemon and oregano suits Greek reds whose ripe fruit and firm tannins match the meat's richness.
  • Imam bayildi: Olive-oil-rich stuffed aubergine with tomato and onion suits a mineral white, dry rosé or soft, low-tannin Greek red.
  • Baked feta with tomatoes: Greek wines with bright acidity balance the salty, tangy feta and sweet roasted tomatoes, and a savoury rosé bridges both.
